Race time prediction

The Riegel formula projects a recent performance onto another distance. It is a statistical estimate with stated limits, not a promise or an assigned goal.

The method

The formula published by Peter Riegel in 1981 is: time2 = time1 × (distance2 / distance1)^1.06. The 1.06 constant (the fatigue factor) captures how pace slows as distance grows. It is the same convention used by Wolf Engine.

The limits, stated

The estimate assumes equivalent preparation across the two distances: if the known race is a 5K and the target a marathon, the result only holds with marathon-specific training. It also assumes a similar course, weather and conditions. The farther apart the distances, the less certain the estimate. It is not a promise of performance nor an assigned goal: it is a reference for reasoning about paces, to be cross-checked with how you feel and the work done.

  • Riegel, P.S. (1981): Athletic records and human endurance, American Scientist 69(3)
