Legs

  • Barbell squat

    Equipment
    Barbell
    Movement pattern
    Squat
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Set the bar in a rack below shoulder height; rest it across the upper back and hold it wider than shoulder width.
    2. Lift the chest, keep the trunk controlled and step out of the rack. The guide describes feet slightly wider than shoulder width.
    3. Bend the hips and knees under control; stand by pressing the feet into the ground. Depth and load require assessment of individual ability.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Do not confuse upper-back placement with resting the bar directly on the neck.
    • Check trunk position through descent and ascent rather than pursuing depth or load at the expense of control.

    Programming

    Squats appear in general programs with different doses. Read sets, repetitions and progression in the selected program: the exercise name does not establish the dose. This is not proof of direct transfer to a particular sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Back Squat.

  • Goblet squat

    Equipment
    Dumbbells
    Movement pattern
    Squat
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Stand with the feet about shoulder-width apart, holding a dumbbell vertically in front of the chest.
    2. Lower into a squat with the elbows close to the rib cage and the back straight, until the hips are below the knees.
    3. Push the feet into the floor to return to standing.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • The dumbbell stays in front of the chest throughout the repetition.
    • Elbows close to the torso and back straight on the way down and up.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Goblet Squat.

  • Leg press

    Equipment
    Machine
    Movement pattern
    Leg push
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Sit with the back and sacrum flat against the backrest. Feet on the platform, toes forward; adjust seat and foot position so the knees are bent about 90 degrees with heels flat. Lightly hold the handles and brace the abdomen.
    2. Exhale and push the platform away by extending hips and knees, heels in contact with the plate, until the knees are extended but relaxed.
    3. Pause briefly, then return slowly by bending hips and knees.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Do not lock out the knees.
    • Hips stay on the seat pad and the low back does not round.
    • Do not let the thighs compress the rib cage on the way back.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Seated Leg Press.

  • Leg curl

    Equipment
    Machine
    Movement pattern
    Knee flexion
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Lie face down on the machine, the knee joint aligned with the lever axis. Pads on the lower third of the lower legs, not at the heel. Hold the handles and brace the abdomen.
    2. Exhale and bend the knees, moving the pad toward the buttocks in a controlled way, without arching the back.
    3. Continue until the knees are bent past 90 degrees or the pads near the buttocks. Pause briefly and return slowly.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Stabilise the low back: the source warns that poor technique leads to excessive use of the low back extensors.
    • If you cannot control the low back, use a lighter load.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Lying Hamstrings Curl.

  • Step-up

    Equipment
    Dumbbells
    Movement pattern
    Lunge
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Start: stand with feet parallel, hip-width apart, dumbbells in hand with palms facing in. Shoulder blades down and back, shoulders not shrugged.
    2. Up: place the right foot firmly on a platform, torso upright and knee aligned over the second toe. Push off with the trailing leg and bring that foot beside the lead foot.
    3. Down: load the lead foot, step the trailing foot back to the floor, then step off with the lead foot. Repeat on the other side.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Knee aligned over the second toe; the foot and ankle should not collapse in or out.
    • Keep the torso upright: the source allows a slight forward lean but avoids excessive movement.
    • Progression given by the source: step up onto one leg only and stay balanced on that leg before stepping down.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Step-Up.

Posterior chain

  • Romanian deadlift

    Equipment
    Barbell
    Movement pattern
    Hip hinge
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Start standing with the bar in front of the thighs, knees slightly bent and back controlled.
    2. Push the hips backwards as the load lowers close to the body, until tension is felt along the back of the thighs.
    3. Return to standing with the bar close to the body. The source does not require it to reach the floor.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Do not treat reaching the floor as compulsory: the described reference is posterior-thigh tension with a controlled position.
    • Do not move the bar away from the body to gain more depth.

    Programming

    This is a hip-hinge pattern. It is not automatically equivalent to the conventional deadlift required by a program: starting position, range and progression rules can differ. The technique entry does not assign personal sets or loads.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Romanian Deadlift.

  • Incline dumbbell press

    Equipment
    Dumbbells
    Movement pattern
    Incline push
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. With two dumbbells, lie on an incline bench set between 45 and 60 degrees, feet firmly on the floor or on a platform. Shoulder blades down and back against the bench. Head, shoulders, hips and feet stay in contact throughout. Closed pronated grip, neutral wrists.
    2. Press the dumbbells together to straight elbows, about over the eyes or slightly higher.
    3. Inhale and lower the dumbbells in a controlled way toward the upper chest, slightly wider toward the armpits, touching the chest without bouncing. Elbows under the wrists.
    4. Exhale and press back to straight elbows, without arching the back.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • The source treats a spotter as important for safety, to hand over the dumbbells and take them at the end of the set.
    • Elbows close to the sides emphasise the triceps more; flared elbows emphasise the pecs more (source statement).

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Incline Chest Press.

  • Push-up

    Equipment
    Bodyweight
    Movement pattern
    Horizontal push
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Place the hands about shoulder-width apart under the shoulders; extend the body and brace the trunk with the abdominal, gluteal and thigh muscles.
    2. Lower under control with the head aligned with the spine, without letting the lower back sag or the hips rise.
    3. Press the floor away to straighten the arms while retaining body control.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Do not let the lower back sag during lowering or pressing.
    • Do not raise the hips to complete a repetition that loses body alignment.

    Programming

    This is a bodyweight horizontal push. Variation and repetition count change the demand: it is not an equivalent bench-press dose merely because it shares a movement category. This entry does not prescribe sets to failure.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Push-up.

  • Dumbbell bench press

    Equipment
    Dumbbells
    Movement pattern
    Horizontal push
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Lie on a flat bench with one dumbbell in each hand and feet supported by the floor or a platform; keep the head, shoulders and buttocks on the bench.
    2. Keep the shoulder blades supported and wrists controlled; lower the dumbbells towards the chest under control without bouncing.
    3. Press until the elbows extend while retaining the points of contact. Agree on spotter assistance for handing over and taking back the dumbbells when needed.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Do not lose the points of contact or arch the lower back to finish the press.
    • Do not let the wrists bend under the load or bounce the dumbbells on the chest.

    Programming

    The source describes a flat dumbbell bench press, not a machine chest press or incline press. If a program prescribes one of those variations, identify any substitution. Sets and loads belong to the selected program, not to the technique entry alone.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Chest Press (dumbbells).

Back

  • Lat pulldown

    Equipment
    Machine
    Movement pattern
    Vertical pull
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Sit at the machine and lock the thighs under the pad. Brace the abdomen and avoid arching the low back. Grip the bar firmly; with arms overhead, depress and retract the shoulder blades. Lean back slightly (no more than about 30 degrees) and keep the head aligned with the spine.
    2. Exhale and start the pull by lowering the shoulder blades first, then pull the bar to the upper or mid chest, driving the elbows down and toward the sides.
    3. Continue until the bar nears or touches the chest, or the elbows stop moving down and start moving back; do not pull further. Pause briefly, then let the bar rise slowly until the elbows are straight.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • No extra backward lean during the pull.
    • Stop when the elbows start moving backwards: pulling further loads the shoulder joint, according to the source.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Seated Lat Pulldown.

  • One-arm dumbbell row

    Equipment
    Dumbbells
    Movement pattern
    Horizontal pull
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Holding a dumbbell in the right hand, place the left knee and left hand on a bench: hand under the shoulder, knee under the hip. Abs braced, back flat, head aligned, shoulder blades down and back. Right arm extended toward the floor without rotating the torso.
    2. Exhale and pull the dumbbell up by bending the elbow and pulling the upper arm back, close to the side, until you cannot lift further without rotating the torso.
    3. Inhale and lower the dumbbell to the start, keeping the back flat and the shoulder retracted.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • No torso rotation or change in spine position.
    • The source suggests training in front of a mirror to check back, shoulder and trunk rotation.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Single Arm Row.

  • Pull-ups

    Equipment
    Bodyweight
    Movement pattern
    Vertical pull
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Start: under the bar, full grip with thumbs wrapped, palms facing away. Gently cross one leg over the other and brace the abdomen. Head aligned with the trunk, wrists neutral, shoulder blades down and back.
    2. Exhale and pull the body up by bending the elbows and driving them down to the sides of the trunk, without swinging.
    3. Pull until the chin is level with the bar or the hands, pause briefly, then lower slowly to straight arms, keeping the shoulder and wrist position.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • No swinging of the body during the pull.
    • The source warns to perform the exercise without compromising the shoulder: follow its shoulder-blade position cues.
    • A narrow palms-forward grip shifts some load from the lats to the upper back.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Pull-Ups.

  • Conventional deadlift

    Equipment
    Barbell
    Movement pattern
    Hip hinge
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Stand behind the bar with the feet about shoulder-width apart, toes slightly turned out and shins almost touching the bar.
    2. Sit the hips back slightly, keeping the back straight and the chest lifted, then bend forward and grip the bar with an over-under grip (one palm up, one down).
    3. Squeeze the bar and press the feet into the floor; keeping the back flat, drive the hips forward to a tall standing position with shoulders back and legs straight.
    4. Return by shifting the weight back into the hips, back straight, letting the knees bend.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • The guide notes that when the movement is done properly the work is felt in the glutes and back of the thighs, not in the back.
    • Keep the back flat from the start to the end of the repetition.
    • Difficulty given by the source: advanced.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Deadlift.

  • Barbell row

    Equipment
    Barbell
    Movement pattern
    Horizontal pull
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Hold the bar with palms down and hinge forward at the hips with slightly bent knees.
    2. Keep the back controlled and pull the bar towards the navel.
    3. Lower slowly until the arms straighten while maintaining trunk position.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Do not turn each pull into a trunk lift: the source describes maintaining the hip-hinged position.
    • Do not drop the load on the return; the guide calls for a slow, controlled lowering.

    Programming

    This is a horizontal pull that also loads the trunk, not an automatic substitute for swimming technique. If a program calls for another row variation, support and demands change: the same muscle-group label does not make them identical.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Bent-over Row.

  • Military press

    Equipment
    Barbell
    Movement pattern
    Vertical push
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Set the bar in a rack at about shoulder height. Grip it about shoulder-width apart, palms facing up.
    2. Duck under the bar, lift it off the rack and rest it across the top of the shoulders, elbows pointing forward.
    3. Step back with the back straight and tall and press the bar directly overhead. Lower it slowly to the shoulders and repeat.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Back straight and tall during the press.
    • Lower the load slowly back to the shoulders.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Standing Shoulder Press.

  • Lateral raises

    Equipment
    Dumbbells
    Movement pattern
    Shoulder abduction
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Start: stand with dumbbells beside the thighs, neutral grip with thumbs wrapped, elbows straight or slightly bent. Feet slightly wider than hip-width or in a split stance. Abs braced, shoulder blades down and back, head aligned with the spine.
    2. Up: exhale and raise the dumbbells up and out to the sides; elbows and upper arms rise together, slightly ahead of the forearms. Past 60-70 degrees rotate the arms slightly upward. Raise until the arms are level with the shoulders, about parallel to the floor.
    3. Down: inhale and lower the dumbbells slowly to the start, elbows slightly bent, rotating the arms slightly downward past the 60-70 degree mark.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Torso upright, no arching of the low back.
    • Neutral wrists, no flexion or extension.
    • The source says the slight external rotation past 60-70 degrees may reduce potential shoulder impingement (the guide's statement, not a risk estimate).

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Lateral Raise.

  • Hammer curl

    Equipment
    Dumbbells
    Movement pattern
    Elbow flexion
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Stand with dumbbells beside the thighs, neutral grip with thumbs wrapped, elbows extended. Split stance, abs braced, shoulder blades down and back, head and neck aligned with the spine.
    2. Exhale and slowly bend one or both elbows until the dumbbell nears the front of the shoulder. In alternating curls the other arm stays down. Torso upright, elbows not moving forward, wrists neutral, shoulders not shrugged.
    3. Inhale and lower the dumbbells to straight elbows, keeping the neutral grip.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Torso upright, no arching of the low back.
    • If the dumbbells cannot rest at your sides without bending the wrists, choose the position that keeps the wrists aligned with the forearms (source guidance).

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

Triceps

  • Triceps pushdown

    Equipment
    Cable
    Movement pattern
    Elbow extension
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Facing the cable machine with the attachment above head height, handle or rope in a full grip. Feet hip-width or staggered. Abs braced, torso vertical, shoulder blades down and back. Elbows at the sides of the trunk, wrists neutral.
    2. Exhale and slowly extend the elbows, pressing the handle or rope toward the floor, keeping torso, shoulders, arms and wrists still.
    3. Continue to extended elbows (without locking out), pause briefly, then let the handle rise slowly until the elbows start to drift forward, around upper chest level.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Watch that the elbows do not drift forward: the source says it recruits the lats and reduces emphasis on the triceps.
    • Do not lock the elbows out at the end.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Triceps Pushdowns.

  • Crunch

    Equipment
    Bodyweight
    Movement pattern
    Trunk flexion
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Lie on your back on a mat, knees bent, feet flat, heels 12-18 inches from the tailbone. Hands behind the head, shoulder blades together and elbows back without arching the low back.
    2. Exhale, contract the abs and core and tuck the chin slightly toward the chest while curling the torso toward the thighs. Focus on bringing the rib cage toward the pelvis with the neck relaxed. Curl up until the upper back leaves the mat and hold briefly.
    3. Inhale and lower slowly, keeping feet, tailbone and low back on the mat.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • The source warns people usually go too fast and use the hip flexors, which tilts the pelvis and raises low back stress.
    • Keep neck and shoulders relaxed.

    Programming

    Sets, repetitions and progression depend on the programme you choose: the exercise name does not set the dose. The card describes the movement and does not show direct transfer to performance in a single sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Crunch.

  • Plank

    Equipment
    Bodyweight
    Movement pattern
    Trunk stability
    Technique and programming

    Movement

    1. Place the forearms with elbows under the shoulders and hands facing forwards; extend the legs and brace the trunk.
    2. Lift the trunk and legs from the floor, keeping them aligned without sagging through the lower back or raising the hips.
    3. Keep breathing during the hold; lower slowly to the floor under control.

    Checks to avoid errors

    • Do not shift the shoulders behind the elbows to change the lever during the hold.
    • Do not hold the breath or replace trunk control with an arched position. Stop the movement if lower-back pain occurs.

    Programming

    This is an isometric hold: time describes only part of the dose. Do not equate ten seconds of planking automatically with ten repetitions of a dynamic exercise. The technique guide does not establish an individual hold duration or demonstrated transfer to a particular sport.

    Editorial summary of the cited technique guide; no frequency of errors or comparative injury-risk claim. ACE, Front Plank.
