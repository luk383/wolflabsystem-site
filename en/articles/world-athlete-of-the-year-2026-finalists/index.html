World Athlete of the Year 2026: the six finalists for Monaco

Races3 min readPublished November 14, 2026

  2. The men's finalists
  3. The women's finalists
  4. Limits
  5. Sources

At a glance

  • World Athletics announced the three men's finalists on 10 November and the three women's finalists on 11 November 2026. The winner will be announced live at the World Athletics Awards in Monaco on 29 November.1,2
  • Men's finalists: Alison dos Santos (400m hurdles world record of 45.80, unbeaten in 11 hurdles races), Mondo Duplantis (pole vault world record raised to 6.31, Ultimate, world indoor and European titles) and Sabastian Sawe (marathon world record of 1:59:30 in London, first man under two hours in a record-eligible marathon).1
  • Women's finalists: Melissa Jefferson-Wooden (100-200 double at the Ultimate Championship, 10.62 and 21.47), Masai Russell (100m hurdles world record of 12.09, unbeaten season) and Audrey Werro (Ultimate, Diamond League and European 800m titles, 1:53.70).2
  • The finalists come from a four-way vote - fans, athletes, media and the World Athletics Family - on ten nominees selected by an international panel of athletics experts.1,2

How the award works

The ten nominees per gender had been selected by an international panel of athletics experts; voting by fans, athletes, media and members of the World Athletics Family cut the list to three finalists per gender. All six will be invited to Monaco for the World Athletics Awards on 29 November, where the winner will be announced on stage. The same evening will also see the men's and women's Rising Star awards, Coach of the Year, the President's Award and, for the first time, the Moment of the Year award.1,2

The men's finalists

Alison dos Santos (Brazil): lowered the 400m hurdles world record to 45.80 and won the Ultimate title in 45.98, the third-fastest time in history; an unbeaten season across 11 hurdles races, including the Diamond League Final.1

Mondo Duplantis (Sweden): improved his own pole vault world record to 6.31 and won the Ultimate, world indoor and European titles.1

Sabastian Sawe (Kenya): won the London Marathon in a world record of 1:59:30, becoming the first man to break two hours in a record-eligible marathon.1 That is the same time anyone racing the Valencia Marathon on 6 December would need to beat to win Juan Roig's one-million-euro prize.

Outside the top three, in order: Josh Kerr (4th), Ja'Kobe Tharp (5th), Kenneth Bednarek (6th), Collen Kebinatshipi (7th), Rumesh Tharanga Pathirage (8th), Andy Díaz Hernández (9th) and Kristjan Čeh (10th).1

The women's finalists

Melissa Jefferson-Wooden (USA): won the sprint double at the Ultimate Championship and set world leads of 10.62 in the 100m and 21.47 in the 200m, the latter putting her third on the world all-time list.2

Masai Russell (USA): 100m hurdles world record of 12.09 and nine of the ten fastest times of the season; Ultimate and Diamond League titles in an unbeaten hurdles season.2

Audrey Werro (Switzerland): Ultimate, Diamond League and European 800m titles; broke the Diamond League record three times, improving to a world lead of 1:53.70, third on the world all-time list.2

Behind them: Tigst Assefa (4th), Marileidy Paulino (5th), Agnes Ngetich (6th), Chase Jackson (7th), Yan Ziyi (8th), María Pérez (9th) and Camryn Rogers (10th).2 Ngetich, sixth among the nominees, is due to make her marathon debut in Valencia on 6 December.

Limits

  • The statistics reported (records, all-time positions, unbeaten seasons) are those of the World Athletics press releases, not our own independent check of results databases.1,2
  • We make no prediction about the winner: the composition and weighting of the vote that will decide the award are not published in the two press releases.
  • The Rising Star finalists (announced on 12-13 November) are not covered in this article.
