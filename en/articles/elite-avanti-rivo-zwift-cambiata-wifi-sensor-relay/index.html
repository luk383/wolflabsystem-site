Elite Avanti and Rivo: virtual shifting on Zwift moves to Wi-Fi, with Sensor Relay

Tech3 min readPublished November 28, 2026
On this page
  1. What changes with the new firmware
  2. Zwift Sensor Relay: accessories go through the trainer
  3. How the update arrives
  4. Limitations
  5. Sources

At a glance

  • Elite has updated the firmware of its Avanti and Rivo trainers: Zwift virtual shifting now works with the trainer connected over Wi-Fi alone, with no more Bluetooth pairing needed for the Zwift Click.1
  • Zwift Sensor Relay also arrives: heart rate strap, cadence sensor and controllers connect to the trainer, which carries them into Zwift over a single connection. On Apple TV, which accepts at most two sensors, the heart rate strap can be used alongside the controllers again.1
  • The update installs automatically over Wi-Fi: the versions stated by Elite are 42 for Avanti and Justo 2 and 24 for Rivo, checkable with the Upgrado app.12

What changes with the new firmware

Elite announced it on its website on 26 November: with the latest update, Zwift virtual shifting works on Avanti and Rivo even when the trainer is connected to Zwift over Wi-Fi, in both the Zwift Ready versions and those compatible with Zwift Cog and Click. Previously the trainer could already join the home network, but using the Zwift Click handlebar controller required a Bluetooth pairing. These are two features the Justo 2 with Zwift Cog and Click, the flagship model, already offered.1

According to Elite the change covers all of its Wi-Fi trainers. The stated benefit is a more stable connection, especially when several riders are racing on Zwift in the same room.1

Zwift Sensor Relay: accessories go through the trainer

The second change is about accessories. With Zwift Sensor Relay, the heart rate strap, cadence sensor and Zwift Click no longer connect to Zwift one by one: they connect to the trainer, which brings them into the app together with its own data. Elite presents it as the same principle as its Cardio and Cadence Bridge function, with the difference that Sensor Relay also works with the virtual-shifting controllers.1

The practical case cited by Elite is Apple TV, which accepts at most two connected sensors at a time: anyone using a trainer and a shifting controller had no room left for a heart rate strap. With Sensor Relay the accessories take up a single connection, the trainer's, and the strap becomes usable again.1

How the update arrives

Justo 2, Avanti and Rivo update themselves over Wi-Fi: if the trainer is already connected to the home network there is nothing to do, otherwise it is configured once from the My E-Training app (Settings, Wi-Fi). The three models support 2.4 GHz networks only; Elite's guide recommends a network name with Latin characters and numbers only and a password no longer than 32 characters.12

The trainer checks for new firmware every four power cycles and downloads it in the background (white LED flashing, trainer usable); installation starts at the next power-on, signalled by the blue LED flashing, takes a few minutes, and during that phase the trainer is not visible to apps. It should stay plugged in until done. For trainers compatible with the Gateway (Justo, Justo 2, Avanti) updates happen over Wi-Fi only, not Ethernet. The installed version is checked with the Upgrado app: the one with these features is 42 for Avanti and Justo 2 and 24 for Rivo.12

Limitations

This article is based on the announcement Elite published on its website on 26 November 2026, on Elite's guide to Wi-Fi setup and automatic updates (updated 23 September 2026) and on Cyclinside's report of 27 November, all read on 28 November 2026. The firmware versions are those stated by Elite at the announcement date; actual rollout timing on individual trainers is not specified. We did not test the update on a device: the procedures described are those documented by Elite. Sensor Relay behaviour with apps other than Zwift is not covered by the sources examined.
