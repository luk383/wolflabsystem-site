Caffeine and performance: doses, timing and when to avoid it

In short

  • Caffeine improves endurance performance consistently, though not dramatically.
  • Effective dose: 3–6 mg per kg of body weight, about 60 minutes before. Below 3 mg/kg the effect is less clear.
  • Test it in training: it can cause stomach upset, jitters and sleep problems.

What the research says

The International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand sums up decades of studies: caffeine reliably improves endurance performance at doses of 3–6 mg per kg taken about 60 minutes before.1 For lower doses, around 2 mg per kg, the position stand says the minimum effective dose is unclear. Towards 9 mg per kg side effects become frequent and such a high dose is not needed. Response varies a lot between people: this is an expert consensus on the studies, not an individual prescription.

It acts mainly on the nervous system: the same effort feels a little easier.

An espresso usually has roughly 60–100 mg of caffeine (a rough estimate, not from the source): content varies a lot. Gels and tablets list the exact dose on the label.

  • Marathon: part before the start and, if you like, a caffeinated gel after halfway.
  • Always test it in training, on a long run at race pace.
  • Afternoon or evening races: watch out for that night's sleep.
  • Avoid it or ask your doctor if pregnant, or with heart rhythm problems, anxiety or high blood pressure.
