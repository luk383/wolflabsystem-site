Nike Vomero 19 and Vaporfly 5: a 2027 preview, two different roles

In short

  • On 16 November Nike previewed two projects for 2027: Vomero 19 for everyday mileage and Vaporfly 5 for racing, with a stated focus from 5K to the half marathon.1
  • Vomero 19 is announced for March 2027. Vaporfly 5 is still a prototype: the final product is planned for fall 2027.1
  • Nike's claimed 17% weight reduction and 8% increase in energy return concern the new ZoomX LT foam compared with ZoomX. They are not figures for the complete shoe or running time.1

Two shoes, two roles: a preview, not a test

The announcement is part of World of Athletes, Nike's showcase of products and technologies for upcoming seasons. The general release describes a first look at the future range, not products that are all already available to buy. For running, the distinction is between a highly cushioned daily trainer and a racing shoe.1, 2

ModelStated useAnnounced release
Vomero 19Everyday trainingMarch 2027
Vaporfly 5Racing, 5K-halfFall 2027; currently a prototype

These are the manufacturer's stated roles and release windows. They do not establish Italian availability, a delivery date or suitability for every runner. WOLFLAB has not tested either model.

Vomero 19: ZoomX and ReactX LT for everyday miles

Nike describes Vomero 19 as an evolution of the 18: it preserves the previous model's geometry and stack height, while aiming to reduce weight and improve breathability. Its midsole combines ZoomX with a new ReactX LT compound. Do not confuse that with ZoomX LT, the material announced for Vaporfly 5.1

Other announced changes include a seamless engineered mesh upper, a redesigned tongue and heel, and a radial outsole pattern. Nike claims lower weight than Vomero 18 and a familiar underfoot feel, but the release gives neither weight in grams nor stack height in millimetres. We do not turn those descriptions into measurements or independent comfort judgments.1

Vaporfly 5: a wave-shaped Flyplate and two foam layers

Nike positions Vaporfly 5 for races from 5K to the half marathon. At its centre is a wave-shaped Flyplate, with a wider midfoot and extended tip. Standard ZoomX sits below the plate, with ZoomX LT above it. The description also includes a Vapor Mesh upper, a moulded ZoomX sock liner and GOATEK rubber targeted at the forefoot.1

The brand describes added cushioning while maintaining the previous model's heel-to-toe offset. It does not provide numerical stack or drop figures here. Phrases such as "the most aggressive Vaporfly yet" are the manufacturer's language, not a result from our lab or a recommendation to use it for a distance beyond the stated brief.1

The 17% and 8% claims concern the foam

The material comparison needs careful reading: Nike claims ZoomX LT is 17% lighter and offers 8% more energy return than ZoomX. The subject is the new foam, not the entire Vaporfly 5. This does not mean a shoe that is 17% lighter, an 8% reduction in the energy cost of running or an 8% improvement in finishing time.1

The release does not detail the comparative measurements' protocol, conditions or uncertainty on this page. We therefore report the numbers as Nike's material claims, not an independent measure of running economy, durability or road performance.

Release plans and missing information

The announced window for Vomero 19 is March 2027, on nike.com and at selected retailers. Vaporfly 5 is explicitly shown as a prototype: the final product is planned for fall 2027. The preview should not be treated as a final specification for the shoe that will go on sale.1

The release does not give Italian prices, absolute weights or numerical stack and drop measurements. We have not verified local availability, size ranges, durability, race approval for the final product or independent test results. The preview helps explain the direction of the two models; it is not a basis for buying today or promising a personal best.

Limits and sources

This report is based on two Nike releases dated 16 November 2026, read in full that day. Both are manufacturer sources, not two independent confirmations of performance. No WOLFLAB test, no ranking of shoes and no manufacturer images reproduced without verified rights.
