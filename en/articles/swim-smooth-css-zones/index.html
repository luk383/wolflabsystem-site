In brief

  • Swim Smooth defines training zones from CSS: the pace you can hold over 1500 m, calculated from 400 m and 200 m time trials. Easy zone: CSS +8–10 s per 100 m. Steady zone: CSS +6 s. Threshold zone: CSS.
  • Their CSS is the organisation’s operational definition and does not coincide with the scientific definition based on the distance-time slope.
  • The source is a blog post from a commercial organisation, partly paywalled. The full zone table is not visible and is not reproduced.

Why CSS instead of heart rate

For the author, heart rate is hard to measure in the pool (straps shift, the wrist is hard to read) and very individual. CSS is specific to swimming, measurable with a stopwatch and responds to training.1

The zones the source makes visible

ZonePace per 100 mStated use

The other zones are in the paid table and are not reproduced.1

Illustrative example

With a CSS of 1:40 per 100 m: steady = 1:46, easy = 1:48–1:50, threshold = 1:40. Numbers chosen to show the calculation, not a prescription.

Testing and progression according to the source

The source suggests retesting every 6–8 weeks, and gives as an estimate an improvement of 0.3–0.5 s per 100 m per week, with a plateau after 8–12 weeks. These are the author’s estimates, not study results: they should not be read as a promise.1

Limits

  • Swim Smooth’s CSS (400 m + 200 m, 1500 m pace) is theirs; the scientific definition uses the distance-time slope. See CSS and critical speed.
  • Commercial source, partly paywalled: the full zone table and the sample sets are not reproduced.
  • No comparison with other zone systems was searched.

Sources
