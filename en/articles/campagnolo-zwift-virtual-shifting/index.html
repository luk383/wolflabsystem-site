Campagnolo on Zwift: virtual shifting comes to Record and Super Record 13

Technology4 min readPublished November 14, 2026
On this page
  1. What changed on 10 November
  2. Requirements, in practice
  3. Is the Zwift Cog required?
  4. Limits
  5. Sources

At a glance

  • Since 10 November 2026, Campagnolo Super Record 13 and Record 13 wireless Ergopower levers, road and gravel, control Zwift's virtual shifting: Campagnolo is the first component maker to support the Zwift Protocol.1
  • You need compatible levers and derailleurs on firmware 3.1.16 or higher (updated via the MyCampy app), Zwift 1.125 or newer, and an updated Zwift Ready or Cog Compatible trainer. No ANT+: the trainer must connect via Bluetooth, USB, Wi-Fi or Ethernet.1,2
  • When the levers are paired to Zwift, physical derailleur shifting is disabled: you can switch between physical and virtual shifting, but not use both at once.1
  • The Zwift Cog is not required if you run a Campagnolo 13-speed cassette, Zwift staff clarifies. The feature is included with a Zwift membership at no extra cost.1,2

What changed on 10 November

Campagnolo and Zwift announced that the Super Record 13 Wireless Ergopower groupset can control virtual shifting when paired with a compatible smart trainer. The update is available now for Super Record 13 and Record 13, road and gravel versions, and makes Campagnolo the first bicycle component manufacturer to support the Zwift Protocol for virtual shifting.1

Virtual shifting replaces physical gears: the trainer changes resistance electronically to match the selected virtual gear, while the chain does not move. Zwift offers 24 virtual gears, shown in the in-game HUD. In the settings you can choose Campagnolo-style or sequential shifting logic; the levers and buttons also send Ride Ons and trigger PowerUps.1,2

Requirements, in practice

  • Super Record 13 or Record 13 Wireless Ergopower levers with compatible derailleurs, updated to firmware 3.1.16 or higher via the MyCampy app.1,2
  • Zwift version 1.125 or newer.1,2
  • A Zwift Ready or Zwift Cog Compatible trainer, updated to its latest firmware.1,2
  • Trainer connected via Bluetooth, USB, Wi-Fi or Ethernet: virtual shifting is not supported over ANT+. The levers use a single Bluetooth LE connection; on Apple TV, if Bluetooth channels run out, the Zwift Companion app can bridge the levers to the game.1,2

Two usage details: before pairing the levers to Zwift, set a low-to-medium physical gear, because pairing disables physical shifting; you can switch between modes, but not use them at the same time. In ERG-mode workouts, virtual shifting only works during free-ride segments while resistance follows the workout targets. The feature can be turned off in the in-game settings and is also supported in ROUVY with compatible Zwift hardware.1,2

Is the Zwift Cog required?

Zwift's FAQ lists the Zwift Cog kit among the options for "Cog Compatible" trainers, which fed the idea that the Cog is mandatory. Replying in the official forum, Zwift staff clarified that the Cog is not a requirement for virtual shifting: the electronics happen between the levers and the trainer firmware, and there is no electronics inside the Cog. Riders already using a bike with a Campagnolo 13-speed cassette on a trainer can keep that setup. One practical caution: do not pair a worn chain with a new Cog, or vice versa, to avoid skipping under load.1,2

Limits

  • The clarification that the Zwift Cog is not mandatory comes from a staff reply in the official Zwift forum: the same staff says the support pages will be updated, but they were not yet aligned at the time of writing.1
  • The general virtual-shifting FAQ contains parts that predate the announcement: the Campagnolo-specific block is the one updated on 10 November 2026.2
  • We did not directly verify firmware 3.1.16 in the MyCampy app or the behaviour on individual trainers: the requirements reported are those stated by Zwift. For groupsets other than Record 13 and Super Record 13, Campagnolo points to future announcements.1
  • No WOLFLAB test: the behaviour described is as stated by Zwift and Campagnolo.
