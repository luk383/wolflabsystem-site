Ironman Cozumel 2026: Sarzilla third in 7:30:33, a new Italian record. Keulen wins

At a glance

  • Michele Sarzilla takes third at Ironman Cozumel in 7:30:33, the fastest Ironman-distance time ever by an Italian, 1:38 quicker than the 7:32:11 Gregory Barnaby set in Sweden in August. The podium also secures his slot at the 2027 IRONMAN World Championship in Kona.123
  • Dutchman Youri Keulen wins in 7:24:34, his first Ironman victory. Germany's Lasse Nygaard Priester is second in 7:30:23 on his full-distance debut, ten seconds ahead of Sarzilla.1
  • Austria's Lisa Perterer retains the women's title in 8:21:56, more than seven minutes faster than her 2025 win.1

Sarzilla's race, leg by leg

On Sunday 22 November in Cozumel, Mexico, Sarzilla comes out of the water with the leaders: 38:01 for the 3.8 km swim, five seconds behind front man Sven Thalmann (37:56, on what Slowtwitch assumes was a current-aided course). He rides the 180 km in 4:08:18, then closes the marathon in 2:39:36, the fastest run split of any pro, quicker even than the winner's 2:40:05.12

The year-on-year comparison shows the step up: twelve months ago, also in Cozumel, Sarzilla made his full-distance debut with a fourth place in 7:55:53. That is 25 minutes slower. His coach Fabio Vedana, quoted by A24 Sport, reports a 44 km/h bike average and eleven minutes gained on that leg over last year. A month after the Kona World Championship his own team described as a half misstep, the Mexican bronze reopens the road to Hawaii for 2027.2

Keulen breaks clear in the marathon

The men's race is decided in the second half of the marathon. Nygaard Priester leads through halfway by twenty seconds, but by 28 km Keulen is 2:30 in front and runs on to win alone. Niek Heldoorn's fourth place in 7:32:30 completes the group of Kona 2027 qualifiers, while pre-race favourite Matthew Marquardt finishes fifth. Eight of the top ten are Europeans.1

Limitations

Results reconstructed from Slowtwitch's race report and results tables dated 22 November, read on the 24th; placings and splits match the official Ironman data quoted by the Italian sources. The previous Italian benchmark (Barnaby, 7:32:11 at Ironman Sweden on 15 August 2026) is cross-checked between the German Wikipedia Bestenliste and TriRating's national records list, both updated in November 2026. Triathlon records are not ratified: courses carry no certified measurement and times across different races are only partly comparable, as TriRating itself notes. Vedana's comments are quoted from A24 Sport, not gathered by us. We did not attend the race.
