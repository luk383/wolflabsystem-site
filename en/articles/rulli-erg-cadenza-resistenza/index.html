ERG mode: why slowing down can make pedalling harder

In short

  • In ERG mode, the trainer tries to maintain the power requested by the app. If cadence falls at the same target, demand per pedal stroke rises: slowing down does not necessarily make an interval easier.1,2
  • Wahoo recommends steady cadence and allowing the trainer time to adjust resistance, rather than chasing each power increase with cadence changes and gear shifts.1
  • This does not mean "never shift in ERG": gearing can prevent the trainer from going low enough during recovery or reaching some low-cadence power targets. The response depends on the model.1
  • ERG Easy Ramp is a feature Wahoo describes for its KICKR trainers and smart bikes. Do not assume every trainer has it.2

ERG and free resistance modes ask different things

Wahoo's SYSTM guide distinguishes ERG and Level modes. In ERG, the app sends a power target and the trainer adjusts resistance to try to hold it; in Level, you reach the watts through gearing and pedalling. Other platforms may name their free resistance modes differently. We use the Wahoo documentation's terms here, without transferring its settings to every app.1

The practical task changes too. In ERG, Wahoo explains, follow the workout's cadence target and let the trainer regulate watts. Accelerating sharply to chase the on-screen number can make applied resistance fall; shifting to reach a new target faster can slow the system's response. Response time varies between trainers: a brief deviation from target is not, by itself, evidence of a fault.1

The spiral: less cadence, more demand per stroke

At constant target power, reducing pedal rotation speed requires more torque. Wahoo explains this as more force demanded per pedal stroke: the trainer increases resistance just as you slow down. If the target is too demanding to maintain cadence, a loop can develop: you slow, resistance rises, you slow further, until you can no longer turn the pedals. The documentation calls this ERG lock-up or the "death spiral".1,2

This is not a measurement of your fitness and does not, alone, establish a mechanical or electronic fault. It is a possible consequence of ERG regulation. For classic lock-up, the guide suggests stopping pedalling and allowing the trainer to release resistance. If it keeps happening, it suggests reconsidering the requested intensity. This is not a universal wattage prescription.1

When gearing really matters

Avoiding constant shifts does not remove the trainer's limits. Wahoo describes a minimum power level for each gear: if the system cannot drop to the recovery target, an easier gear may lower that floor. This can happen on both wheel-on and direct-drive trainers.1

The guide suggests the small chainring and middle of the cassette as a starting point, adjusted to recovery targets. This is not a universal setup. In the opposite situation, when the trainer cannot reach a low-cadence interval's power target, a harder gear may be needed. Wahoo mentions the large chainring, noting that this may then affect recoveries and does not apply to every trainer.1

The useful distinction is between shifting to chase every transition and choosing a gear compatible with the workout's power range. Before applying a fix, check your model's guide and how the app controls the trainer. Support for virtual shifting does not automatically solve an ERG problem: they are different functions. See our report on Campagnolo and virtual shifting on Zwift.

Easy Ramp: what Wahoo describes

In its ERG Easy Ramp documentation, Wahoo describes resistance falling gradually when cadence drops below a threshold. Once cadence returns above that threshold, the system gradually brings resistance back to target over about 10-15 seconds. Its stated purpose is to ease restarting without immediately imposing full resistance at low RPM.2

This describes the feature on Wahoo KICKR trainers and smart bikes. It is not a guarantee for every trainer or combination of app and firmware. The page gives no numerical cadence threshold. We do not invent one or promise an exact restart time for every configuration.2

What to check before blaming the trainer

  • Mode: are you in ERG or a mode where you regulate watts yourself? They do not work the same way.1
  • Transition: are you holding cadence or accelerating and shifting to chase the target immediately? Allow your system's response time.1
  • Gear and limits: is recovery power staying too high, or is a low-cadence power target out of reach? These need different checks.1
  • Model: check features and restart procedures in the manufacturer's documentation. Not every trainer responds alike.1,2

Limits

This is a guide to Wahoo documentation updated on 24 October 2026, not a WOLFLAB comparative test. We have not measured response times, accuracy or the behaviour of individual trainers. Guidance for SYSTM, KICKR and Easy Ramp does not certify compatibility or identical behaviour on other platforms. No mode, by itself, guarantees better fitness.
