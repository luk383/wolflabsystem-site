AIU bans: three years for Jepchirchir and Loleo over EPO, six for Korir. What changes

In brief

  • On 19 November the Athletics Integrity Unit (AIU) announced bans for three Kenyan athletes: Peres Jepchirchir, reigning world marathon champion and 2021 Olympic gold medallist, three years for EPO use; Veronica Loleo, three years, also for EPO; Ronald Korir, six years for unexplained biological passport abnormalities.1
  • Jepchirchir signed an admission of the violation and accepted the disqualification of all results since 17 July 2026, including her Sydney Marathon win. The race organiser announced that the title will go to Irine Cheptai once the decision becomes final, after a 30-day appeal window.12
  • Loleo loses her half marathon silver from the World Road Running Championships in Copenhagen. The team-standings recalculation, which could hand gold to the United States, is not official yet: the World Athletics page still shows Kenya on top (checked 20 November).13
  • Korir, fourth at the 2023 Berlin Marathon, was charged over abnormal passport values and, having failed to respond to the charge by the deadline, is deemed to have admitted the violation: results disqualified from 19 January 2024.1

The three AIU decisions

The AIU statement, issued from Monaco on 19 November, describes three separate cases closed through different procedures.1

Peres Jepchirchir, 33, admitted breaching rules 2.1 and 2.2 (presence and use of a prohibited substance). An out-of-competition sample collected in Kenya before the Sydney Marathon, which she won on 30 August, returned an adverse finding for EPO on 17 September 2026; the provisional suspension followed on 20 October. In her AIU interview she denied using EPO, but later signed the admission form, accepting the disqualification of all results since 17 July 2026. The standard four-year ban was cut to three for the early admission. Jepchirchir's career wins include New York 2021, Boston 2022, London 2024 and Sydney 2026, on top of Olympic gold and the world title.14

Veronica Loleo, world number six in the half marathon and third at the Berlin Half Marathon in March, returned adverse findings from blood and urine samples collected out of competition in Kenya on 6 September 2026. The AIU issued a notice of allegation on 23 October with an immediate provisional suspension. She said she did not know how the finding occurred and mentioned an injection, whose name she could not recall, to treat an injury. She then signed the admission, accepting the disqualification of results since 6 September 2026, including the silver at the World Road Running Championships in Copenhagen. Four years reduced to three for her too.1

Ronald Korir, 35, fourth at the 2023 Berlin Marathon, is the only one of the three without a positive test: the case stems from the biological passport. Twenty blood samples collected between 6 May 2023 and 18 August 2025 produced abnormal values that the expert panel judged to indicate "likely doping" in two periods: January to March 2024, before the Hamburg Marathon, and January/February 2025, before the Osaka Marathon. Summoned by the AIU, Korir twice failed to provide the requested written explanations; charged on 25 September 2026, he did not respond by the deadline and is therefore deemed to have waived a hearing and admitted the violation. Six years, with results disqualified from 19 January 2024, the date of the first abnormal sample.1

Sydney: the title goes to Cheptai, but not yet

The TCS Sydney Marathon announced that Irine Cheptai will be crowned 2026 champion once the AIU decision becomes final: the procedure includes a 30-day appeal window and until then the official results stand. Under the reallocation, Cheptai would win in 2:22:11, ahead of Shure Demise Ware (2:22:33) and Haven Hailu Desse (2:22:57). The organiser notes that no prize money is paid before testing results are cleared, and that prizes will follow the amended standings. It would be Cheptai's first World Marathon Major win. One detail from the Sydney statement: Jepchirchir tested negative on race day, in a sample analysed by the WADA-accredited Sydney laboratory, underlining the role of out-of-competition testing.2

Copenhagen: the team recalculation is not official

Loleo's ban cancels her second place in the half marathon at the World Road Running Championships in Copenhagen and changes the team standings, which are based on the times of each nation's first three athletes. According to the recalculation published by Runner's World, without Loleo's time Kenya would drop from 3:17:33 to 3:22:04 and gold would pass to the United States (3:19:51 with McClain, Kelati and Hurley). As of 20 November, however, the official World Athletics page still shows Kenya leading with 3:17:33: any medal reallocation remains subject to an update that has not yet been published.35

The context: Kenya towards the 2029 Worlds

AIU chief executive Brett Clothier acknowledged the Kenyan authorities' progress over the past five years while stressing that "there is still a long way to go": the Nairobi government has committed to strengthening the national anti-doping agency and acting against the criminal networks facilitating doping. In September, Kenya was awarded the 2029 World Athletics Championships, the first edition in Africa; according to the BBC, more than 140 Kenyan athletes have been suspended by the AIU since 2017, and WADA president Witold Banka said the country must show it can be a "clean" home of athletics ahead of the event. Jepchirchir is the second high-profile Kenyan sanctioned in two years after Ruth Chepngetich, banned for three years in October 2025: her marathon world record (2:09:56, Chicago 2024) still stands, because the disqualification did not cover that result.146

EPO (erythropoietin) is a prohibited substance in category S2 of the 2026 WADA list, banned at all times: it stimulates red blood cell production, increasing oxygen transport to the muscles, but remains detectable in testing only for a short window, which explains the weight of surprise out-of-competition controls.14

Limitations

This article is based on the official AIU statement of 19 November, read in full, and on the TCS Sydney Marathon statement: we do not have access to the complete individual disciplinary decisions, which the AIU had not yet published in its first-instance decisions list at the time of writing (page last updated 22 October). The athletes' statements quoted here (Jepchirchir's initial denial, the injection mentioned by Loleo) are those summarised in the AIU statement. The Copenhagen team-standings recalculation is a Runner's World reconstruction, not an official figure: the standings published by World Athletics, checked on 20 November, are not yet updated. The Sydney title reallocation has been announced by the organiser but takes effect only once the decision is final. The Kenyan context figures (more than 140 suspensions since 2017, the 2029 Worlds award) and the status of Chepngetich's record come from the BBC and Reuters, not from a database consulted directly.
