New York City Marathon: five boroughs, one course

In short

  • The 2026 TCS New York City Marathon is on Sunday 1 November 2026, according to NYRR, the organiser.
  • The 2026 course covers five boroughs: it starts on Staten Island, passes through Brooklyn, Queens, Manhattan and the Bronx, and returns to Manhattan for the finish in Central Park.
  • This is the course only. We give no paces, split times or predictions: the sources we read do not publish them.

The course in one line

Staten Island, Brooklyn, Queens, Manhattan, the Bronx, Manhattan again. That is the sequence shown on NYRR's official 2026 course map, with the Queensboro Bridge between Queens and Manhattan, Harlem and the Bronx further on, and the finish in Central Park.4 The official 2026 course page says the 26.2-mile race runs through the five boroughs.3

For the start and the kind of course we rely on the NYRR 2025 runner guide, that is the previous edition: the start is on Staten Island at the foot of the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge and the finish is in Central Park.2 We did not read an equivalent 2026 guide, so we do not attribute other details of that guide to the 2026 edition.

Why five boroughs

According to the 2025 NYRR guide, the race began in 1970 with 127 runners circling Central Park and went citywide in 1976. The guide says 2026 marks 50 years of the five-borough course.2 In 2024 the marathon had more than 55,000 finishers and was the largest in the world, again according to the 2025 guide.2

Date and race page

  • According to the 2025 guide, the race is held on the first Sunday in November.2 In 2026 that is 1 November.1
  • Our TCS New York City Marathon page covers the next edition with entry, 7 November 2027, with fee, drawing and how to get there.

Limits

  • The start and history come from the 2025 guide. The 2026 course page still has some sections that refer to 2025, so we do not report times, road closures, aid stations or other race-day details.
  • We did not read the elevation profile in numbers, so we do not say how much the course climbs or where the hardest stretches are.
  • This is not a race guide or a pacing plan. For rules and spectator information use the NYRR site.
