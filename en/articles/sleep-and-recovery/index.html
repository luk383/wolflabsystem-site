Sleep and recovery: the cheapest performance gain

In short

  • Adults need at least 7 hours a night; people who train a lot often need more.
  • Extending sleep for a few weeks improved performance in the athletes studied.
  • With irregular hours, consistency and a short nap help more than any supplement.

What the research says

Sleep medicine societies (AASM and SRS, 2015) recommend that adults sleep at least 7 hours a night on a regular basis, for general health.1 An expert consensus focused on athletes (published online November 2020) adds that many elite athletes sleep less than they need, especially during heavy training, travel and early-morning races.2

A review of sleep loss reports that performance got worse in much of the research, but findings conflict and it is unclear how large the effect is or how it works.3 Conversely, in a study of 11 college basketball players, extending sleep for 5–7 weeks compared with a baseline period improved speed and shooting accuracy.4 The sample is very small and from one sport, so it cannot be generalised to amateur runners: the direction is plausible, not proven for you.

In practice

The tips below are general sleep-hygiene practice, not results of these studies.

  • Regular wake-up times, even on days off, as far as possible.
  • A 20–30 minute nap in the early afternoon after a short night.
  • Hard sessions not too late: intense evening training can delay falling asleep.
  • Dark, cool bedroom, screens away in the last half hour.
  • No caffeine in the 6–8 hours before bed.
  • Heavy weeks: if you're sleeping much less than usual, cut intensity before you cut sleep.

How we reviewed it

Document review of the 4 cited sources: a joint recommendation by scientific societies and an expert consensus (L5), a review with mixed results, and a study of 11 basketball players.

Limits. Very small sample from a single sport. It does not prove the effect for recreational runners. The practical tips are good practice, not study results.
