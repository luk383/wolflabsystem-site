Sleep and recovery: the cheapest performance gain

Strength and recoveryModerate evidence4 min readPublished October 26, 2026

In short

  • Adults need at least 7 hours a night; people who train a lot often need more.
  • Extending sleep for a few weeks improved performance in the athletes studied.
  • With irregular hours, consistency and a short nap help more than any supplement.

What the research says

Sleep medicine societies recommend at least 7 hours a night for adults.1 An expert consensus focused on athletes adds that many sleep less than they need, especially during heavy training, travel and early-morning races.2

When sleep is short, perceived effort, mood and the ability to handle hard training all get worse.3 Conversely, in a study of college basketball players, extending sleep for several weeks improved speed and accuracy.4 The studies are few and small, so the evidence level is moderate, but the direction is clear.

In practice

  • Regular wake-up times, even on days off, as far as possible.
  • A 20–30 minute nap in the early afternoon after a short night.
  • Hard sessions not too late: intense evening training can delay falling asleep.
  • Dark, cool bedroom, screens away in the last half hour.
  • No caffeine in the 6–8 hours before bed.
  • Heavy weeks: if you're sleeping much less than usual, cut intensity before you cut sleep.

Sources

  1. Watson and colleagues. Joint American Academy of Sleep Medicine and Sleep Research Society recommendation on sleep duration for adults. Sleep, 2015. PubMed
  2. Walsh and colleagues. Sleep and the athlete: review and 2021 expert consensus recommendations. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2021. PubMed
  3. Fullagar and colleagues. Review of the effects of sleep deprivation on performance and recovery. Sports Medicine, 2015. PubMed
  4. Mah, Mah, Kezirian, Dement. Effects of sleep extension on college basketball players. Sleep, 2011. PubMed
