2026 Tauranga World Cup: four Italians on the start line, Crociani and Missaglia hunting the podium

In brief

  • The World Triathlon Cup makes its Tauranga debut on Sunday 22 November in New Zealand: four Italians start the sprint-distance race. Women off at 14:00 local (2:00 CET), men at 16:00 local (4:00 CET).1
  • Alessio Crociani is the only man in the field with two WTCS podiums in 2025 (Hamburg and the Wollongong Finals); Carlotta Missaglia arrives off back-to-back fourth places at World Cups (San Pedro de la Paz and Iquique). Alongside them, Samuele Angelini and Pietro Giovannini.324
  • Men's favourites: John Reed (USA, top seed), Roberto Sanchez Mantecon (ESP) and Izan Edo Aguilar (ESP), silver in Miyazaki six days ago. Women: Márta Kropkó (HUN, No. 1) and home favourite Nicole Van der Kaay (NZL), second at the T100 in Dubai last Saturday.23
  • Sprint course at Pilot Bay: a 750 m beach-start swim, 20 km on the bike over four laps, 5 km run over two laps. Live on TriathlonLive.2

A new venue with an eye on the future

Pilot Bay hosts a triathlon World Cup for the first time: Tauranga will then stage a T50 World Championship event in 2027 and the T50 Championship Finals in 2028, so Sunday's race is also a first look at the course that will decide world titles. The start lists count 60 men (11 New Zealanders) and 49 women.23

The format is the sprint: a one-lap 750 m swim from the beach, four 5 km bike laps on a flat, technical circuit through Mount Maunganui, then two 2.5 km run laps along The Mall and Marine Parade, in the shadow of Mauao.2

The Italians: Crociani and Missaglia lead the way

The Italian federation confirms four starters: Alessio Crociani (Fiamme Azzurre), Samuele Angelini and Pietro Giovannini (Fiamme Oro) in the men's race, Carlotta Missaglia (Carabinieri - Valdigne Triathlon) in the women's. All four appear on the official start lists published by World Triathlon.14

Crociani, wearing number 15, is according to the official preview the only man in the field with two WTCS podiums in 2025: bronze in Hamburg and at the World Championship Finals in Wollongong. His 2026 has been disrupted, but 12th place at last weekend's Finals in Abu Dhabi suggests he is finding his way back. Missaglia, number 6, finished fourth at back-to-back World Cups in October and November, in San Pedro de la Paz and Iquique: the federation's preview lists her among the athletes to watch.23

The favourites

In the men's race the top seed is John Reed (USA), a World Cup winner and WTCS medallist expected to close his season on the podium; behind him Roberto Sanchez Mantecon (ESP) and above all Izan Edo Aguilar (ESP), who lost gold only in the finishing sprint in Miyazaki six days ago. Also watch Mitch Kolkman (NED), third in Rio in August, and Márton Kropkó (HUN), the 2025 aquathlon world champion.3

In the women's race the number 1 belongs to Márta Kropkó (HUN), twice a World Cup silver medallist in 2026 (Edmonton and Beijing), but current form favours New Zealand's Nicole Van der Kaay, second at the T100 in Dubai less than a week ago and already a World Cup winner at home in New Plymouth in 2023. Zuzana Michalickova (SVK) won the Rio World Cup in August and arrives off bronze in Miyazaki.2

How to follow

The starts fall in the middle of the Italian night: women at 2:00 CET, men at 4:00 CET on Sunday 22 November. The race streams live on TriathlonLive; official results will be published on the event pages once the races finish.14

Limitations

This is a preview based on the FITRI announcement, World Triathlon's two official previews and the start lists published by the international federation, not on our presence at the venue. Accounts of past results and the favourites are those of the official previews. Start lists can change until the race begins. Italian start times come from the conversion stated by FITRI (14:00 and 16:00 local, New Zealand). TriathlonLive availability may depend on subscription and region.
