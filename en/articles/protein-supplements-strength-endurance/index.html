Protein supplements: what they add to food for strength and endurance

Supplements5 min readPublished October 31, 2026
On this page

  3. Endurance
  4. Limits and product
  5. How we reviewed it
  6. Sources

In brief

  • A protein supplement is a convenient food, not an ergogenic aid: it adds something only if your total daily protein is low.
  • In strength training a meta-analysis of 49 studies (1,863 people) finds small effects: about 2.5 kg more in maximal strength and 0.3 kg of lean mass. The effect falls with age and is larger in people who are already trained.
  • Beyond a certain total daily amount (about 1.6 g/kg/day in that meta-analysis) there was no further gain in lean mass. This is one study's result, not a prescription.
  • For endurance, carbohydrates come first: protein may help reduce muscle damage and aid recovery, per the ISSN position stand.

What they add

Strength exercise and ingested protein both stimulate muscle protein synthesis, and they act synergistically when protein is taken before or after training.2 The Morton et al. meta-analysis (2018) confirms a small but significant benefit of supplementing during strength programmes of at least 6 weeks in healthy adults: maximal strength +2.49 kg (95% CI 0.64-4.33), lean mass +0.30 kg (0.09-0.52).1 These are group averages: they do not say how much you will gain.

The daily total matters more than the product

  • Threshold: with supplementation, total intakes above about 1.6 g/kg/day gave no further lean-mass gains in the meta-analysis.1 It is not a prescription and does not hold for every person or situation.
  • Age and training: the effect on lean mass falls with age and is larger in people with training experience.1
  • Timing: for the ISSN the best timing is probably a matter of individual tolerance, since benefits come both before and after; the anabolic effect of training lasts at least 24 hours but tends to fade with time.2
  • Food or powder: the ISSN recognises that requirements can be met from food; a supplement is a practical way to secure quality and quantity without too many calories, for example with high training volumes. It also advises favouring foods with all the essential amino acids.2 The AIS treats isolated protein as a sports food, not as an ergogenic aid.4

Endurance

For runners, cyclists and swimmers the ISSN position stand says the priority is adequate carbohydrate, and that adding protein may help reduce muscle damage and aid recovery.2 It is not proof of better race performance. For race carbohydrate use Fuel. On BCAA and EAA see the supplements map; free-form EAA are covered there.3

Limits and product

  • The sources concern healthy people who train. With medical conditions, in pregnancy or with medicines, see a doctor.
  • One author of the meta-analysis declares funding from the National Dairy Council, which supported trials included in the analysis: this is a declared conflict of interest.1
  • Anti-doping and contamination: the WADA list changes every year and an unchecked product can contain banned substances.56 WOLF LAB does not certify products or batches and does not recommend brands.
