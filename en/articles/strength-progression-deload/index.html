Start with a readable rule

Progression means changing the training stimulus over time according to a criterion, not adding weight every session regardless. Published programs use different criteria: completed sets, weeks, tests or training max. The ACSM 2026 synthesis supports progressive training, but does not establish one optimal calendar for everyone. [1]

What is changing?

VariableWhat to record
LoadWeight and the rule for increasing it
Repetitions or setsPlanned and completed work, warm-up separate
EffortRIR or the scale used, not just an adjective
Frequency and contextStrength sessions and other training during the period

Changing every variable together makes results harder to interpret. This is a diary-reading principle, not proof that one progression format is superior. See volume, intensity and RIR for definitions.

Deload and taper are not synonyms

The 2023 Delphi consensus defines deloading as a period of reduced training stress intended to limit physiological and psychological fatigue, support recovery and prepare for subsequent training. A taper instead targets performance at an approaching competition. Both can reduce stress without having the same purpose or place in the plan. [2]

Consensus is not proof of the optimal dose

The study involved 34 coaches in round one, 29 in round two and 21 in round three. It gathered and compared expert views in strength and physique sports. It did not assign athletes to different deload weeks to measure which worked best. Describing common use of roughly a week does not establish that everyone needs a deload every four weeks. [2]

Reduction can involve sets, repetitions, load or proximity to failure. An isolated percentage does not describe total stress and does not become a personal prescription here. Choices depend on program and context; the consensus does not verify a universal dose.

A volume example, not a deload protocol

Changing one exercise from 4×6 to 2×6 halves planned sets and repetitions, from 24 to 12. At the same 50 kg, volume-load changes from 1,200 to 600 kg. Warm-up excluded. The calculation does not prove a 50% fatigue reduction or guarantee recovery: load, exercise, effort and the rest of the week can change the picture.

This arithmetic example separates quantity from response. It is not advice to halve your own program.

For people who run, ride and swim

Reducing gym work while substantially increasing running, cycling or swimming does not necessarily reduce overall stress. Read the whole calendar before calling a week a deload. This study does not demonstrate that strength-sport consensus criteria transfer to every multisport athlete. [2]

For protocols actually studied in endurance sports see running, cycling and swimming. Persistent decline, pain or symptoms are not automatically fixed by naming a week a deload. The problem needs understanding, not a label.

Sources and limits

  2. Bell et al., Integrating Deloading into Strength and Physique Sports Training Programmes, 2023. Coach Delphi consensus, not an experimental dose study.
