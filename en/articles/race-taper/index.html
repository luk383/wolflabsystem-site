The pre-race taper: how much and how to cut back

In short

  • In studies the most effective taper lasts about two weeks.
  • Volume drops by 40–60%, progressively.
  • Intensity and number of sessions stay almost the same: cutting everything costs sharpness.

Why taper

During a build, fatigue hides part of your fitness. Cutting the load in the final days lets fatigue clear while the adaptations stay: that's why you feel "light" after a well-run taper.

What the research says

A meta-analysis of 27 studies of competitive athletes (not only marathoners) identified the most effective pattern: about two weeks, with volume cut by 41–60% progressively, keeping intensity and session frequency.1 Lowering intensity or frequency did not improve results. These are averages across studies: an individual may respond differently.

An earlier review reports an average improvement of about 3% (usual range 0.5–6%), with tapers from 4 to over 28 days and volume cuts of up to 60–90%: the patterns used in studies vary widely. It is not a guaranteed gain in a marathon.2

In practice, for a marathon

The table is a practical adaptation of these findings, not a protocol tested on the marathon.

WhenVolumeWhat to keep
14–8 days outabout 70–75% of normalOne marathon-pace session, long run cut to 22–26 km
7–3 days outabout 50%A short race-pace touch, for example 3 × 2 km
Last 2 daysminimalShort jog with a few strides, carb loading
  • Same number of runs as before, just shorter.
  • No heavy strength work in the last 10 days.
  • The bike stays only as a short, easy spin if it helps loosen the legs.
  • Feeling heavy mid-taper is normal: don't add sessions to "make up".
