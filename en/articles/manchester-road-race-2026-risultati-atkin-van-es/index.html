Manchester Road Race 2026: Atkin wins on debut in 20:57, three seconds off the record. Van Es first Dutch winner

At a glance

  • On Thursday 26 November, Thanksgiving morning, the 90th Manchester Road Race was held: a hilly 4.737-mile loop (about 7.62 km) through downtown Manchester, Connecticut, with almost 15,000 registered.23
  • Sam Atkin (Great Britain, 33) won on debut in 20:57 after dropping the field on the downhill third mile: the second-fastest time in race history, three seconds off Edwin Kurgat's course record (20:54 in 2025). Kurgat finished second in 21:01, Morgan Beadlescomb third in 21:03.12
  • Diane van Es (Netherlands, 27) won the women's race in 24:03, twenty seconds clear: according to Race Results Weekly she is the first Dutch winner of this race. Florencia Borelli and Annie Rodenfels tied in 24:23.12

The race

The 90th edition of the Manchester Road Race was run on Thanksgiving morning, Thursday 26 November, under gray skies, on a 4.737-mile downtown loop in Manchester, Connecticut. The sources we read report almost 15,000 registered runners.12 The profile is rolling: the second mile climbs Highland Street, where the "King of the Hill" and "Queen of the Hill" primes are awarded; then the course turns left onto Porter Street and plunges downhill. The finish on Main Street is uphill.1

Men's race: the downhill move

Kurgat, the defending champion and course record holder, led over Highland Street and collected the $1,000 "King of the Hill" prime. With him in the lead group were Atkin, David Mullarkey, Dawit Seare and Beadlescomb. After the left turn onto Porter Street, Atkin made his move: a 4:17 third mile (which included an uphill portion early on) opened a six-second gap, and a 4:10 fourth mile stretched it to ten. By the final turn onto Main Street the win was safe.1

Atkin broke the tape in 20:57 with his hands raised: an average of 4:25 per mile, about 2:45 per kilometre. It is the second-fastest time in race history, three seconds off Kurgat's record; according to Athletics Weekly he is only the fourth man ever under 21 minutes on this course. He collected the $10,000 winner's prize, while the $2,000 course-record bonus went unclaimed.12

Behind him, Kurgat won the sprint for second over Beadlescomb: 21:01 to 21:03. Beadlescomb told reporters he "popped something in my ribs" at the final turn and never got his speed back. Dawit Seare was fourth in 21:11: Race Results Weekly describes him as the reigning world road running champion over 5 km, and Athletics Weekly reports he won that title ahead of Jakob Ingebrigtsen. Mullarkey was fifth in 21:16.12

Atkin said he had slept "probably 45 minutes" because of a headache and had shown up simply to "test the body". At six foot two, he said a long stride helps on the downhill, and that he was not wearing a watch.1

Women's race: van Es alone in front

Diane van Es was first at the "Queen of the Hill" prime and by the four-mile mark she led Florencia Borelli (Argentina) and Annie Rodenfels (United States) by 16 seconds. She closed in 24:03, twenty seconds clear: for Race Results Weekly she is the first Dutch winner of the Manchester Road Race. Borelli and Rodenfels tied in 24:23.12

The course record still belongs to Weini Kelati (22:55 in 2021), a five-time champion who did not race this year; Race Results Weekly notes that 24:03 is one of the slower winning times in race history. According to World-Track, van Es is the European 10,000 m silver medallist (Rome 2024, 30:57.24, a personal best) and holds the Dutch road records over 10 km (30:29) and 5 km (14:33).13

The numbers

20:57 over 4.737 miles (about 7.62 km) works out at 4:25 per mile, roughly 2:45 per kilometre. Kurgat's record (20:54) is about 2:44 and a half per kilometre: Atkin lost three seconds, most of them on the final climb where Kurgat closed out his own sprint for second. Atkin's fastest splits were the third mile (4:17) and the fourth (4:10). In the women's race, 24:03 is about 3:09 per kilometre.1

Who Sam Atkin is

Athletics Weekly traces the Briton's path: from Grimsby, college at Lewis-Clark State College in Idaho, now based in Colorado. The Tokyo Olympics ended with a DNF in the 10,000 m because of an Achilles problem; Paris was marked by food poisoning and then Covid, with an 18th place in his 5,000 m heat; then six months out with chest and back pain before returning in 2025. His personal bests are 12:54.66 for 5,000 m (second on the UK all-time list behind Mo Farah), 27:26.58 for 10,000 m, plus British records over 5 km on the roads (13:16) and the indoor 3,000 m (7:31.97).2

Limitations

This article is a synthesis of three journalistic sources, all in English and read on 28 November 2026: we did not consult the race's official website or the official timing database. The result lists we report go as far as the positions quoted by the sources; times beyond the first five men and the first three women were not checked one by one. The quoted remarks are those reported by the journalists on site. Every source we read gives the distance as 4.737 miles; we did not check the course measurement certificate.
