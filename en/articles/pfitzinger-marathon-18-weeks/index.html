Pfitzinger: the 18-week marathon plan, session categories and first weeks

In brief

  • The Pfitzinger and Douglas marathon plan lasts 18 weeks, for people running 50–70 miles a week (80–110 km): four phases (endurance, lactate threshold plus endurance, race preparation, taper).
  • Sessions fall into eight categories. For the authors, long runs and threshold sessions matter most, then VO₂max.
  • The source is a 2001 magazine article by the same authors. It is a plan for runners who already have a base: at least 40 miles a week.

Who it is for

The plan described is for mid- to high-level marathoners running 50 to 70 miles a week (about 80–110 km). The authors say to run at least 40 miles (about 64 km) a week before starting and to have recently completed a run close to the first week’s long run. With a much smaller base they suggest a base programme first.1

The four phases

Endurance, lactate threshold plus endurance, race preparation, taper. The plan is written day by day, with the category of each session, so the balance of the week is visible. You can move a day, but lost work should not be made up with several hard days in a row.1

The eight session categories

CategoryDistance or durationHow it is described
Long run17 miles or moreBuilds from a slow start to about 20% slower than marathon pace at mile 5, then about 10% slower for the last 5 miles. Not a slow jog, according to the authors.
Medium-long run11–16 milesReinforces the long run, at a similar pace.
General aerobicup to 10 milesSteady, moderate pace to build volume.
Lactate thresholdtempo of 4–7 miles, at least 20 minutes at threshold paceClose to 15K to half marathon race pace; slower runners nearer 15K. After a 2–3 mile warm-up.
VO₂maxrepeats of 600 to 2000 mAt 95–100% of VO₂max pace, close to 3K–5K race pace; for marathoners the authors specify 5K pace.
Speedrepeats of 50 to 150 mFor leg speed and form, with plenty of rest, often at the end of an easy run.
Recoveryshort runRelaxed pace, clearly slower than the other workouts.
Marathon pacemedium-long or long run with most of it at goal pacePractises race pace and form.

Why few speed sessions

The authors write that they built the plan around the systems that most determine marathon success, in this order: endurance, lactate threshold and VO₂max. For them long runs and tempo runs matter more than track repeats.1

Illustrative example

A week like the first has 53 miles, about 85 km: the long run is 15 miles (24 km) and three days out of seven are recovery or rest. It is not a plan to copy: it shows how the source distributes the work.

Limits

  • One magazine article, from 2001, by the authors themselves. The book Advanced Marathoning and current plans are not among the sources: they may differ.
  • It is a plan for high volumes. For lower volumes the authors point to other plans in the book, not reproduced here.
  • The source consulted does not report a controlled study comparing this plan with others.
  • The plan is for the road marathon.
