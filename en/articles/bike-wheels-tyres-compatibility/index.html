Bikes, wheels and tyres: compatibility first

The system comes before the component

Do not choose a wheel on weight and rim depth alone. Frame, fork, axles, brakes, freehub, cassette and tyre need to work together. This is a verification path: a size printed on one component does not approve the complete assembly. The model-year manuals remain the reference.

Geometry: medium is not a universal size

Stack is the vertical distance from the bottom bracket to the top of the head tube; reach is the horizontal distance between those references. They compare frames, not the complete hand position. Stem, spacers, handlebar and levers change the contact point. Canyon’s guide, updated in March 2026, distinguishes frame measurements from effective cockpit measurements. It does not prove that one geometry suits everyone. [1]

Before changing bikes, compare the actual size and configuration rather than the endurance or race label alone. This guide does not infer a personal frame size from height or replace a position assessment.

Checks before buying a wheel

CheckModel-specific information
Frame and forkWheel diameter, tyre clearance, axle and hub spacing
DrivetrainExact freehub and cassette, any specified spacers
BrakesBrake type, rotor interface and permitted diameters
TyreSize approved for internal rim width and rim type
LimitsSystem pressure, total mass and intended use

Zipp’s manual gives limits for particular wheel/hub combinations and includes bike, rider and equipment in system weight. A limit for one model does not apply to every wheel in the range. This table does not confirm any specific pairing. [2]

Hookless: a tube does not make a tyre compatible

For its TSS system, Zipp requires TSS-approved tyres even when an inner tube is fitted. The list is updated and also refers to the tyre manufacturer’s declarations. The stated ceiling is 5 bar, but a size or model may require a lower limit: this is not a pressure recommendation for every rider. The tyre’s minimum pressure must also be compatible with the system. [3]

Schwalbe requires its TLE/TLR tyres on hookless rims, including when used with tubes, and says to observe the limits of the combination. A tubeless label alone does not establish compatibility between every rim and every tyre. If instructions conflict, do not fit parts by trial and error: obtain confirmation of the pairing from the manufacturers. [4]

What this guide does not compare

It does not assign watt savings to a rim depth, select personal tyre pressure or rank brands. Assessing a purchase requires the exact model, intended use, configuration and verifiable data. A lighter or deeper wheel does not fix an incompatible interface.

Sources and limits

  2. Zipp, Road Wheels manual. Technical documentation: limits depend on the model.
  3. Zipp, Hookless Tire Compatibility. Dynamic manufacturer list, not approval of every pairing.
  4. Schwalbe, Hookless Rims. Requirements for Schwalbe tyres.
