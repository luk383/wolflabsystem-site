StrongLifts 5×5: structure, progression and limits

Strength and recovery6 min readPublished October 29, 2026

In brief

  • Full-body program, three sessions a week, two alternating sessions (A and B): squat, bench or overhead press, row or deadlift.
  • Add weight when all sets are completed. Otherwise repeat the load.
  • The source consulted does not experimentally evaluate this program. It is a routine described by its author, with a commercial site and app.

Structure

Session ASession B
Squat 5×5Squat 5×5
Bench press 5×5Overhead press 5×5
Barbell row 5×5Deadlift 1×5

Three sessions a week (usually Monday, Wednesday, Friday), with at least one rest day between sessions, alternating A and B.1 5×5 means five sets of five reps at the same load, with about 3 minutes of rest. The deadlift is a single set of five after warm-up.1 Order: legs, push, pull.

How you progress

If you complete five reps on every set of an exercise, you add weight next time. If not, you repeat the load until you complete it. The rule applies exercise by exercise.1 Increments given by the source: 5–10 lb (2.5–5 kg) for squat and deadlift, 2.5–5 lb (1.25–2.5 kg) for bench, overhead press and row.

Illustrative example

An example in kg, inspired by the one in the source. It is not a prescription.

ExerciseLast sessionNext session
Squat5×5 at 60 kg, all completed5×5 at 62.5 kg
Overhead press5/5/5/3/3 at 40 kg5×5 at 40 kg (repeat)
Deadlift1×5 at 80 kg, completed1×5 at 82.5 kg

Starting weights

The source advises starting light: someone who has never lifted starts with the empty bar on squat, bench and overhead press. Someone with experience starts from a load they could lift for 10 reps.1

Limits

  • The source is the author's own site, which sells an app and spreadsheets. It describes the program and does not evaluate it with a study. Studies cited by the site are not a direct test of the specific 5×5 routine described here.
  • The program is meant for people starting or returning. Fixed increments stop working when progress slows: variants for that phase are outside this sheet.
  • With a lot of running or cycling, three sessions with squats and deadlifts weigh on recovery. No source read evaluates this program for runners or cyclists: compatibility not assessed. For the general evidence see Strength for runners and cyclists.
  • Several versions share the name “5×5”: do not confuse it with Bill Starr’s 5×5.
