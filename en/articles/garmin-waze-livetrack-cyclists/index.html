Garmin and Waze: real-time cyclist alerts and what you actually share

Gear3 min readPublished November 11, 2026
On this page
  1. How it works
  2. What you actually share
  3. Why it is news
  4. Limits
  5. Sources

In brief

  • On 10 November 2026 Garmin and Waze announced "Live Cycling Alerts": riders on a compatible Garmin Edge bike computer or smartwatch can appear as a moving icon on the Waze map of nearby drivers.1,2
  • It works through LiveTrack with a smartphone and the Garmin Connect app: sharing is opt-in for each outdoor activity, temporary, and switches off automatically when the ride ends.1,2
  • Drivers only see a temporary alert that a cyclist is nearby: never personal data or the route, according to Waze.2
  • The rollout is announced as global on Android and iOS, but the compatible model list is not published in the releases and actual availability per country is unverified.1,2

How it works

With a compatible Garmin Edge or smartwatch, a smartphone and the Garmin Connect app, riders who turn on LiveTrack for an outdoor cycling activity can choose to share their location with Waze. Drivers using the Waze app see nearby Garmin cyclists as active moving icons on the road, with a notification when a cyclist is close.1,2

LiveTrack itself is not new: it is Garmin's free feature that lets family and friends follow your location, speed and course in real time. This collaboration extends the same feed to Waze drivers.1

What you actually share

Sharing is opt-in: you enable it from Garmin Connect when you start the activity, it lasts only for the session, and it turns off by itself when you stop recording on the device.1,2

On the driver's side, Waze says motorists only see a temporary alert that a cyclist is nearby: never information about the specific cyclist or their route.2

The LiveTrack premise still applies: while it is on, your real-time location flows from Garmin's systems to Waze. If you do not want to share it, leave the feature off - nothing changes without the opt-in.1

Why it is news

Both companies present the feature as a tool for mutual awareness between cyclists and drivers: Garmin speaks of giving drivers "real-time visibility" of cyclists, Waze of a reminder to slow down and share the road.1,2 These are company statements: there is currently no independent evidence that alerts of this kind reduce crashes, and they should not be read as a safety promise.

Limits

  • The releases say "compatible Edge bike computer or Garmin smartwatch" without listing models. Before counting on your device, check Garmin's official documentation: at the time of writing we found no dedicated support page with the list.1
  • "Global rollout" is Waze's wording: we have not verified actual availability for individual accounts or countries, Italy included.2
  • Any restrictions by road type or area are not published in the two sources.
  • The road-safety claims are company statements, not measured results.
