Critical swim speed (CSS): what it is, how it is measured and where it stops being valid

In brief

  • Critical swim speed (CSS) is the slope of the line linking distance and time across several maximal trials. It is not a laboratory measure of threshold.
  • The value depends on the protocol: one review found 16 different protocols in 41 studies. Change the distances and the result changes.
  • A calculation can be mathematically valid and still implausible. That is why data should be checked before trusting a number.

What it is

The idea comes from Wakayoshi and colleagues (1992): plot the distance swum against the time taken over several maximal trials and the points sit almost on a line. The slope of that line is the critical speed.1 With two trials, for example 200 m and 400 m, the formula is simple: CSS = (D2 − D1) / (T2 − T1), in metres per second.

What the reviews say

A review of 41 studies found 16 different protocols for calculating CSS. In young national-level swimmers the 200 m and 400 m pair was repeatable (typical error up to 0.04 m/s, variability below 4%), but the authors consider it a crude method suited to in-season checks. Better precision needs at least three trials. Distances of 50 and 100 m are excluded and the suggested trial duration is 2 to 15 minutes. Triathletes and amateurs are not covered by that review.2 Another study of swimmers concludes that the D′ parameter (the distance capacity above CSS) is not reliable.3

Checks on the data before using a result

These are arithmetic and consistency checks, not physiological thresholds:

  • The long trial must take more time than the short one, over a longer distance. Otherwise the denominator makes no sense.
  • CSS should come out below the mean speed of both trials. That only happens if the long trial is swum at a lower mean speed than the short one. If CSS comes out higher, the times are not coherent: recheck the figures or repeat the test.
  • A positive slope is not enough. A number can come out of the formula with wrong times or trials done under different conditions (pool, start, form on the day).
  • Trials must be done the same way (pool, type of start, rest between trials), because the protocol changes the result.

What we do not do

We do not publish a CSS calculator or swim zones: the site currently has no swim test or data, and a zone derived from two times without the checks above would be a number without a criterion. If a calculator is added it will carry the consistency checks and the limits written here, and stay an indicative estimate.

What we know and what we do not

We know how it is calculated, that the result depends on the protocol, and that the 200 m and 400 m pair is repeatable in a group of young competitive swimmers. We do not know how well it holds for amateurs and triathletes, or whether zones built on CSS improve training in those groups.
