Maffetone's MAF method: the 180 formula and its limits

In brief

  • Phil Maffetone's MAF method uses a maximum aerobic heart rate: 180 minus age, adjusted for health and training.
  • The idea: train the aerobic base below that heart rate, to avoid moving into anaerobic work.
  • According to the authors of a 2020 review, the link between training at this heart rate and performance still has to be verified scientifically.

The formula

Subtract your age from 180, then adjust for your situation.1

SituationAdjustment
Major illness, surgery, hospital stay, rehabilitation, regular medication, stage 3 overtrainingminus 10
Injury, no progress in tests, more than two colds or infections a year, allergies or asthma, overfat, stage 1 or 2 overtraining, inconsistent training or a restartminus 5
Consistent training (at least 4 times a week) for up to two years, without the problems aboveno adjustment
More than two years without problems, with progress in MAF tests and racing, no injuryplus 5

Exemptions: above 65 further individualisation may be needed (up to 10 beats more, only for the category with more than two years of training). Up to 16 years the formula does not apply and 165 has been used.1

Illustrative example

A 40-year-oldMAF heart rate
Returning to training after a break180 − 40 − 5 = 135
Training consistently for under two years180 − 40 = 140
Training for over two years, with progress and no injury180 − 40 + 5 = 145
Recovering from a major illness180 − 40 − 10 = 130

Example numbers to show the calculation, not a prescription.

Why, according to the author

Maffetone writes that he linked heart rate, posture and gait: at higher intensity, more anaerobic work and more mechanical distortion. From measurements on athletes he derived a heart rate that promoted aerobic function without much stress. He says this rate was often lower than the one given by 220 minus age. This is his explanation, not a demonstration.1

What the literature says

In 2020 Maffetone and Laursen published a review of the program in Frontiers in Physiology. In the abstract they write that the relationship between training at this heart rate, laboratory measures of fat oxidation, health outcomes and athletic performance “must be verified scientifically”.2 Maffetone is the author of the formula: the review is not independent.

Limits

  • The sources are the author's site and a review co-authored by him. I did not read controlled studies of the formula and the abstract says they are missing.
  • The formula gives one number per person. The adjustment categories rely on self-assessment.
  • The source talks about endurance athletes in general. For running paces and distances, the Daniels sheet and the intensity-distribution entry describe other approaches: they should not be merged.
  • People with heart problems or on medication should ask a doctor: the formula does not replace that.
