San Sebastián Marathon 2026: Fouad's comeback in 2:19:39, Saussez wins the women's race

At a glance

  • Yazami Mohamed Fouad wins the 47th San Sebasti\u00e1n marathon in 2:19:39, catching and dropping long-time solo leader Denis Kipkoech. Youness Assehli is second in 2:20:17 and local runner Unai Arroyo third in 2:20:24.12
  • Amelie Saussez takes the women's race in 2:35:29 with an attack after 30 km. Britain's Tracy Barlow finishes second in 2:36:36 and Maitane Melero third in 2:39:11.12
  • The race was held on Sunday 22 November in wind and occasional showers. Across the marathon and 10K, 8,341 runners from 75 countries finished; the Z Zurich Foundation donates one euro per finisher to the AEFAT association, 8,341 euros in total.1

Fouad, a comeback built kilometre by kilometre

The men's race has one leader for more than three quarters of the course. Denis Kipkoech goes hard from the gun and runs alone: halfway in 1:07:08, with a lead of more than a minute and a half at 30 km (1:36:34 against the 1:38:07 of his closest chaser). Past the 30-kilometre wall, fatigue and wind begin to tell.1

Behind him, Yazami Mohamed Fouad paces his race to perfection: 26 seconds down at 35 km (1:55:04), he completes the pass before 40 km (2:11:57) and reaches the Boulevard finish line alone in 2:19:39. Youness Assehli finishes second in 2:20:17; Unai Arroyo takes third in 2:20:24 and is also crowned Gipuzkoa champion, a title awarded within the race. The EFE news agency describes the same race pattern, with Fouad sitting about ten seconds behind the Kenyan before his final push.12

Saussez settles it in the last ten kilometres

The women's race follows a similar script. Vivian Jelagat Rop leads from the start, passes halfway in 1:16:27 and is still in front at 30 km (1:49:16). Amelie Saussez, running second a few seconds back, attacks shortly after: by 35 km her lead is over three minutes (2:08:13) and she closes out the win in 2:35:29. Tracy Barlow is second in 2:36:36 and also wins the masters-40 category; Maitane Melero completes the podium in 2:39:11. The EFE report places the decisive move around kilometre 32.12

10K, local titles and the day's numbers

The morning 10K goes to Ekain Larrea in exactly 30:00, ahead of Enaitz Aginagalde (30:13) and Be\u00f1at Bengoetxea (30:24). Sara Alonso wins the women's race in 33:16, followed by Frida Michold (34:34) and Mia Steytler (35:59). The women's Gipuzkoa title goes to Nerea Egia in 2:49:03, who also receives the award for the best Donostia runner; the men's local award goes to Borja Barragu\u00e9 in 2:33:50.1

The event, Spain's second-oldest marathon, describes itself as the only race in Spain's Las 5 Grandes circuit with no elite appearance fees. That is the organiser's own claim, not an independently verifiable figure. Finishers numbered 5,141 out of 6,829 starters in the marathon and 3,200 out of 4,048 in the 10K.1

Limitations

This article reconstructs the 22 November results from the organiser's official report, read on 24 November, checked against the EFE wire story and Ruta Running's transcription of the Sportmaniacs results. The tables show the first six finishers; positions four to six rest on a single secondary source. Some outlets report final times differing by one second (EFE, for example, has 2:19:40 and 2:35:30). We use the organiser's official times, including Nerea Egia's 2:49:03 against the 2:49:02 in the Sportmaniacs transcription. We did not attend the race or independently check the timing.
