2026 Cross Duathlon Worlds: De Smet and Maksimava win in Abu Dhabi, Italy's Saravalle sixth

In brief

  • Thibaut De Smet (BEL) retains the title he won in Pontevedra 2025: 1h13'29" on Hudayriyat Island. Silver for Jan Chrzanowski (POL), 25" back, who also takes the U23 world title; bronze for Maxim Chane (FRA) at 27", ten seconds ahead of Sebastien Carabin (BEL).12
  • Hanna Maksimava (BLR) wins the women's gold in 1h25'22", two days after bronze at the Cross Triathlon Worlds: she led at every checkpoint. Silver and the U23 title for Svetlana Chizhikova (RUS) at 1'37", bronze for Sofiya Pryyma (UKR).12
  • Alessandro Saravalle, the only Italian elite on the start line, finishes sixth in 1h15'47", 2'18" behind the winner, with the fifth-fastest mountain bike split.24
  • The elite format: a 6.3 km run, 19.7 km on mountain bikes including a sand dune climb, and a final 3.1 km run. Air temperature at the start: 22.7 °C.2

The format and the conditions

The 2026 World Cross Duathlon Championships took place on Thursday 19 November on Hudayriyat Island, Abu Dhabi, on day six of the Multisport World Championships, with elite, U23, junior, para and age-group titles on the line. The elite distance: two laps of running for 6.3 km, two mountain bike laps for 19.7 km on rolling terrain including a climb up a sand dune, and a final 3.1 km run. The men started at 8:45 local time (5:45 CET), the women five minutes later. The official results page records an air temperature of 22.7 °C at the start; World Triathlon's race report describes temperatures climbing through the morning.123

Men's race: De Smet untouchable, a sprint for bronze

De Smet went clear early with Morocco's Mohamed Nemsi, then rode and ran solo for much of the bike leg and the second run: the official page has him in front at every checkpoint from the first run to the finish. Behind, the Polish pair Jan Chrzanowski and Wojciech Kopyciński ran together about 40 seconds down, with Carabin and Chane closing on the bike.1

In the second run Chrzanowski posted the fastest split (10'40") and the quickest transition, finishing 25 seconds behind the winner: elite silver and, as a 2003-born athlete, the U23 world title. Chane only dropped Carabin at the very end: bronze for France by ten seconds, the Belgian fourth, Kopyciński fifth, Nemsi eighth after his early break. Fourteen finishers out of fifteen starters in the elite classification, which also lists the U23 athletes.12

Women's race: Maksimava unchallenged

With none of the 2025 medallists on the start list, a new champion was guaranteed. Maksimava and Chizhikova shook off the closest chaser, Ivana Kuriackova, early on; the Belarusian racked her bike with a cushion of over 90 seconds and ran the fastest final split to win in 1h25'22", two days after her cross triathlon bronze on the same island. Chizhikova (born 2004) took elite silver and the U23 title; Pryyma won the duel for bronze with Kuriackova, fourth. Eight finishers in the elite classification, which also lists the U23 athletes.123

Saravalle sixth

The only Italian elite in the FITRI delegation, Alessandro Saravalle finished sixth in 1h15'47", 2'18" behind De Smet: seventh after the first run (22'10"), the fifth-fastest mountain bike split (39'45"), eighth on the final run (11'57"). For the Aosta Valley athlete of TriSports VdA it was a return to the cross duathlon Worlds three years after the Ibiza 2023 bronze, which followed his Targu Mures 2022 bronze and a European bronze in Riva del Garda. In 2026 he won his second consecutive Italian title in the discipline and, in early March, world gold in the winter triathlon relay in Padola with Axelle Vicari.245

Limitations

This is a report based on the official results and World Triathlon's race coverage, not on our presence at the venue. The published classifications list U23 athletes within the same elite ranking, as they also contest their category title; the U23 titles mentioned (Chrzanowski and Chizhikova) come from the official race report. The junior, para and age-group races in the same programme are not covered by this article. The 22.7 °C figure is the start-time reading on the official page; the description of rising heat and the dune climb comes from World Triathlon's report. Saravalle's career record is reconstructed from local Aosta Valley press, not from a federation archive consulted directly.
