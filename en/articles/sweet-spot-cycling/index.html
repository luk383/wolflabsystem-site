Sweet spot on the bike: what it is, what is not shown

In brief

  • “Sweet spot” is a coaching convention: usually 88–94% of FTP, between upper tempo and lower threshold. It is not a physiological threshold of its own.
  • According to the guide consulted, it is not proven that it gives “90% of threshold’s benefit for much less fatigue”.
  • The source is a guide from a coaching site, not a study. It notes that FTP depends on how it was measured.

What it means

Sweet spot means sustained work just below threshold, usually at 88–94% of FTP. That band crosses the upper part of “tempo” and the lower part of “threshold” in the most common power models: it is a practical label, not a measured boundary of metabolism.1

What is not shown

  • That it is a distinct physiological zone.
  • That it gives 90% of threshold’s adaptation at a fraction of the fatigue. According to the guide, research usually compares whole training distributions, not that band in isolation.
  • That you need two or three sessions a week: no rule fits everyone.

The guide says this explicitly and cites two meta-analyses (Rosenblat 2019, Filipe 2025). They are reported here as the guide’s citations: the studies were not re-read.1

How the source describes it

PointWhat the source says
Starting pointA recent FTP, with the measurement method named. The same percentage means different things depending on the test and how fresh you are.
IntervalsLong enough to keep power, breathing and technique steady. For beginners: three repetitions of 8–10 minutes near the lower end of the band.
ProgressionExtend controlled time first, then raise power. You do not need to progress every week; reduce after illness, a hard race, travel or poor sleep.
FrequencyOne controlled session is a conservative start. A second only if the first stays repeatable and recovery normal. It is a coaching progression, not a rule.
When to changeIf you cannot recover, if the event asks for something else, or if repeatable performance stops improving.

Illustrative example

With an FTP of 250 W the 88–94% band runs from 220 to 235 W. A session like those the source describes for beginners would be 3 × 10 minutes near 220 W, with easy pedalling between repetitions. These numbers are chosen to show the calculation, not a prescription: the source gives no recovery length.1

Limits

  • One source, from a coaching site. No independent search for studies on this power band was made: these statements are the guide’s.
  • The percentages depend on the FTP used. See FTP and power zones.
