Running training shoes: how to choose.

What to look at in a neutral training shoe, and three models to start from.

What to look at

  • Weight: a lighter shoe helps at lively paces; a heavier one usually offers more structure.
  • Stack and softness: more stack and softer foam protect against impact, but can feel less stable.
  • Where you land: heel or midfoot/forefoot changes which shoe works best, as the energy-return measurements show.
  • Use: easy miles, faster sessions, long runs.

Three starting points

  • adidas Adizero Evo SL: Lightstrike Pro midsole with no carbon plate, light. In RTINGS tests, cushioning and energy return are high for the weight.
  • ASICS Novablast 6: FF BLAST MAX foam with FF TURBO SQUARED pods under the forefoot. Very cushioned. In RTINGS tests the forefoot rebounds more than the heel.
  • Brooks Ghost 18: Classic neutral trainer with DNA Loft v3 foam. In RTINGS tests the platform is very stable and the heel protected, but it is heavy with little energy return.
