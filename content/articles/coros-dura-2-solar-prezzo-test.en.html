In short

  • COROS unveiled the Dura 2 Solar in early November: a solar-charging GPS bike computer succeeding the 2024 Dura. In Italy it is sold on the official store at €499.00 without a mount and €549.00 with the standard aluminium mount, with shipping stated at 3-4 weeks.24
  • The claimed numbers: 120 hours of GPS battery life (70 hours in dual-frequency mode), plus up to 2 hours of solar charge per hour of direct sunlight; 99 g, a 3.5" MIP touchscreen, IP67, and voice, haptic-button and touch controls.1
  • In the first independent test we read (Road Trail Run, road and autumn gravel rides), GPS tracks follow reference devices and battery life in normal use is called "phenomenal", but the backlight is weak in shade and voice control is of little use in the wind.3
  • Watch the box contents: at €499 no handlebar mount is included. The back, however, has a quarter-turn interface compatible with existing Garmin-type mounts.32

Price and availability in Italy: the store check

We checked the official Italian buy page today, 18 November, around 14:30 CET. The Dura 2 Solar comes in two configurations: €499.00 without a mount ("Senza supporto") and €549.00 with the standard aluminium mount. The store states shipping in 3-4 weeks: it is not in stock for immediate dispatch right now.2

One price-relevant detail: the base box contains no handlebar mount at all, as the first independent test also confirms. The back of the unit carries a quarter-turn interface compatible with the Garmin standard, so riders who already own such a mount can use it without buying anything else.3

What COROS claims

The battery is the core of the product: 120 hours of GPS runtime on a single charge in all-systems mode, 70 hours in dual-frequency GPS mode, plus a monocrystalline solar panel adding up to 2 hours of runtime per hour of direct sunlight. USB-C charging takes under 2.5 hours. COROS markets it as having "the longest battery life in the world" among bike computers: that is a manufacturer claim, not an independent measurement.12

The build pairs anodised aluminium alloy with carbon fibre: 99 g claimed, a 5.7 mm top profile, IP67 water resistance. The 3.5-inch Memory-in-Pixel touchscreen (anti-glare coating) is 30% larger than the first Dura's, with 17% higher pixel density. Controls come three ways: touch, two haptic buttons built into the solar panel, and voice commands (start and end rides, intervals, route recalculation, music control).1

Connectivity: up to 15 simultaneous Bluetooth and ANT+ connections (power meters, heart-rate straps, radar, tyre-pressure sensors), with syncing to Strava, Zwift, Ride with GPS and TrainingPeaks. Global maps run on the Ambiq Apollo 510 processor, with route recalculation and automatic climb detection even without a loaded route.1

The first independent test: what holds up and what does not

The most complete test published so far is Road Trail Run's (7 November), based on road and autumn gravel rides. It is a single tester's work: we report it as hands-on experience, not laboratory measurement.3

What impressed. The measured weight (99.9 g against 99 g claimed) confirms the build; recorded GPS tracks follow reference devices even in narrow, forested valleys; the screen reads very well in full sun, and battery life in normal use is described as "simply phenomenal": a device "you'll basically forget to charge". Automatic climb detection shows gradient and elevation gain even during free riding.3

What did not. The backlight feels weak in low sun or rapid light-shade changes, with contrast dropping. Rerouting still requires a smartphone paired over Bluetooth: no phone, no new route, and the algorithm sometimes suggests gravel detours even with the road profile active. Voice control works when stationary, but the tester judged it barely usable in riding wind, and it still needs a button press to activate. The haptic button feedback is almost entirely lost through thick winter gloves. The verdict: high-end hardware for long distances and bikepacking on pre-planned routes, but the navigation software has to catch up, and at €499-549 it is competing with the very top tier.3

Limits

WOLFLAB has not tested the Dura 2 Solar. Battery hours, the solar-charging ratio and the "longest battery life in the world" line are manufacturer claims, not independently verified in this article. The cited test is one reviewer's experience over a limited number of rides, not an instrumental measurement of GPS accuracy or battery life. Prices were observed on the official Italian store on 18 November around 14:30 CET and can change; the stated 3-4 week shipping means availability is not immediate.
