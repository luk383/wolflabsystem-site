At a glance

  • Torino City Marathon and the 42nd Estra Firenze Marathon take place on Sunday 29 November 2026.
  • At our 23 November check, Turin lists online registration as closed, with Village registration on 27-28 November. The first version's online deadline of 20 November has passed.1
  • Florence: the organiser announced a 42K sell-out. The published ordinary 10K deadline was 19 November and has passed. We have not checked exceptions or remaining entries on ENDU.2

Turin: online closed, Village information

The official homepage, read on 23 November, says online registration is closed and lists registration at the Green Pea Marathon Village on 27-28 November. We no longer present the fee valid until 20 November as a current option.1

The race document published on FIDAL's website schedules the start for 08:30 on 29 November in Piazza Vittorio Veneto and the finish in Piazza Castello, subject to organisational requirements. Bib collection is listed at GreenPea, Via Ermanno Fenoglietti 20, on Friday 27 and Saturday 28, from 10:00 to 20:00. Follow your personal confirmation letter and any later organiser notices too.3

Florence: separate sell-out notices from deadlines

The official 27 August announcement reports a sold-out 42K with 13,000 entries and lists 19 November as the ordinary deadline for the 10K, subject to its 3,000-entry cap being reached earlier. That date must no longer be presented as a future deadline. The 10K page confirms the 29 November programme, with an 08:45 start in Via Tornabuoni; it does not itself establish that registration has reopened.24

FIDAL's 25 November note confirms the edition's numbers: 13,000 marathoners at the start (sold out since late August), 50% from abroad and 27% women, across nearly 20,000 participants in the full event programme. Sunday's schedule: the "Maratonabili" start at 08:25, the marathon at 08:30 from Piazza Duomo, the 10K at 08:45 from Via Tornabuoni, the first finishers expected around 10:40, the awards at 11:15 and the time limit running to 14:30. The free pacer service covers bands from 2h55 to 6h00, meeting in the corrals by target time. The expo village is at the Leopolda station; race highlights air on Rai Sport HD on Monday 30 November at 22:50.5

Turin weather: a forecast, not conditions

Yr's hourly table for Turin, checked on 27 November at 6 pm, shows about -1 °C at 8 am and 0 °C at 9 am on Sunday 29 November, with no precipitation, light wind (1 m/s) and almost complete cloud cover. The start is at 8:30, between two full hours in the table: this is a generic forecast for the city, not for the course or the exact time, and it may change before race day. We report no forecast for Florence: the hourly page we had flagged showed data updated on 20 November.6

Turin's field and Davide Re's debut

FIDAL's race-eve note of 27 November confirms a record entry: over 11,000 across the three events, with more than 4,200 in the marathon, over 5,200 in the half marathon (about a thousand more than 2025) and over 2,000 in Saturday 28 November's City Run at the Valentino park (about 5 km). The marathon doubles as the individual and club Regional Championship.7

In the men's race, the fastest personal best among entrants belongs to Moldova's Maxim Raileanu (2:09:22 in Seville in 2024); Kenya's Samson Kipchirchir Keiyo (2:12:52, winner in Ravenna 2024 and Verona 2025) is also a contender. Among the Italians, FIDAL names Francesco Carrera (a 2:20:06 best he means to lower), Daniele Ferronato and Giovanni Susca. In the women's race, Kenya's Viola Jelagat Yator (2:32:27, second in Padua in April) and Ethiopia's Kassaye Agimas Wubu (2:33:10) start in front; the Italians include Gloria Giudici and Barbara Bressi.7

The race is also Davide Re's marathon debut: the former Italian 400 m record holder left the track at the end of last season and, according to FIDAL, lives in Turin, where he has resumed his doctorate in medicine; on Sunday he races for the first time as a father, after his daughter was born on 26 November. We covered him in a dedicated article.7

On the course, FIDAL's note confirms the shared start in Piazza Vittorio Veneto and lists separate finishes: Piazza Castello for the marathon, the Viali di Nichelino for the half.7

Florence favourites in Marathonworld's preview

For Florence we report the names from Marathonworld's 25 November preview: it is a journalistic selection, not an official start list. In the men's race, James Kipkogei Kutto's course record (2:08:40, 2006) is the target of Kenyans Kosmas Kiplimo Lagat (2:08:14 in Seville 2016) and Silas Sugut Kipkoech (2:08:22 in Košice 2025); behind them are Ethiopia's Siyum Diriba Tola (2:11:05 in Riyadh 2026) and, among the Italians, the 2023 Italian marathon champion Francesco Agostini (2:13:53 in Hamburg 2024). In the women's race Lonah Chemtai Salpeter's record (2:24:17, 2018) stands further off: the favourites are Lucy Chepoghon Chelele (2:27:18, second in Florence in 2024), Tsehay Gebre Getiso (2:27:22) and Patience Jebet Kimutai (2:29:53); for Italy, the 2023 national silver medallist Alessia Tuccitto (2:39:23) and Marta Bernardi, at her fourth start here.8
