A watt does not describe the whole session

Power describes mechanical work per unit of time at the point of measurement. It is not perceived effort, does not directly measure metabolic cost and does not certify fitness. Useful interpretation starts with how the number is produced, how comparable it is and which test set the training zones. [1]

Accuracy and repeatability are different questions

The power-meter review includes 74 studies and distinguishes properties such as accuracy, repeatability and sensitivity. A device can be consistent across repeats while differing from the reference. A brand’s percentage claim is not independent validation in every condition. Studies use different references and averaging windows, so they do not yield a universal product ranking. [1]

Meter versus trainer: no fixed percentage correction

Different readings do not prove that one device is faulty. Measurement location, drivetrain losses, recording alignment and conditions can all contribute. The power-duration review stresses consistent devices and procedures. There is no universal correction that converts trainer output into pedal power. [1][2]

Follow the warm-up, zero-offset or calibration procedure in the specific device manual. Record a device change in the training diary rather than automatically treating old records as equivalent. Not every device needs the same procedure.

Power and heart rate: external load and internal response

Heart rate can change during a session at similar power. The review of cardiovascular responses to heat describes effects of skin temperature, intensity and cardiac filling. Rising heart rate alone does not prove poor fitness or dehydration, nor is maintaining watts a reason to ignore heat. [3]

Record context, duration and perceived effort alongside watts when comparing sessions. This contextual reading is not a diagnostic test or a universal decoupling threshold.

From the number to the zones

FTP, critical power and best power over a duration have distinct protocols and models. They are not interchangeable names for the same number. For test procedures and zone conventions, see FTP and power zones. Improvement over five minutes does not automatically establish the same improvement over race duration. [2]
