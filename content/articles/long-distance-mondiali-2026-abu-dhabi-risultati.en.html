In brief

  • Marjolaine Pierré (FRA) won the Long Distance World Championship in Abu Dhabi: a third title in four years, after a duel with Caroline Pohle (GER) settled in the second half of the 30 km run. Silver for Pohle more than 3 minutes back, bronze for Anne-Sophie Pierre (FRA) 22 minutes down.1
  • In the men's race a first world title for Wilhelm Hirsch (GER), in front from the swim. Antonio Benito (ESP), the defending champion chasing a three-peat, pulled up injured at the start of the run after working with Hirsch over the 120 km bike.2
  • Silver for Will Draper (GBR) more than 5 minutes back, bronze for Poland's Wojciech Kopyciński; Bendix Madsen (DEN) withdrew with a foot problem, Ondrej Kubo fourth despite a tumble.2
  • The Multisport World Championships race was held on Hudayriyat Island over 3 km of swimming, 120 km on the bike and 30 km of running, in searing heat.23

Pierré, a third title earned halfway through the run

Caroline Pohle led out of the 3 km swim, 12 seconds up on Pierré after the first lap, but the Frenchwoman halved the gap over the first 30 km bike lap and moved past towards the end of the second of four loops. The German, fourth at the T100 in Dubai only a week earlier, clung on between 15 and 30 seconds back deep into the run and even drew level at the start of the 30 km on foot: then the elastic snapped, with Pierré 13 seconds clear after one lap, 23 after two and more than a minute up by halfway. Pierré stayed relentless to the line for her third world title in four years.1

Behind them, Anne-Sophie Pierre dropped teammate Aurelia Boulanger early in the run and finished third, 22 minutes down, just weeks after a top-10 in Kona. Boulanger was fourth, Lydia Dant (GBR) fifth.1

Hirsch dominates, Benito stops: the three-peat chase ends

In the men's race Wilhelm Hirsch and Antonio Benito broke away in the water, with over two minutes on the first chasers after the 3 km swim and six minutes on Draper and Madsen. The pair worked together across the 120 km bike, while Madsen pulled out with a foot injury picked up during race week. Early in the run, though, Benito pulled up injured: his pursuit of a third consecutive title ends without a finish.2

Hirsch ran the six final laps alone and took the tape more than five minutes clear of Will Draper, himself three minutes ahead of Kopyciński, bronze for Poland. Ondrej Kubo was fourth despite a dramatic tumble at the dismount, Toon Marien (BEL) fifth and Louis Woodgate sixth. "The gap was quite comfortable but it was tough, especially over the last two laps," said Hirsch, who explained he had spoken with Benito before the race about building a margin together.2

Limitations

This is a results piece based on the two official race reports published by World Triathlon on 22 November, read in full. The international federation's articles do not report absolute finishing times, and at the time of writing the full classifications with times were not yet published on the official results and timing pages: the gaps quoted (more than 5 minutes, more than 3 minutes, 22 minutes) are those given by the official reports. The exact nature of Benito's injury is not specified by the source. We were not present at the venue.
