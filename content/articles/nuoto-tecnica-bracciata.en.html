In brief

  • Studies describe technique through stroke rate, stroke length and arm coordination, and look at how they change with fatigue.
  • There is no "right" stroke rate or stroke count valid for everyone: they depend on height, speed and distance. We publish no target values.
  • This page is a short guide with a low evidence level: we read only the abstracts of the sources.

What is measured

A swimming review summarises studies on three things: assessing aerobic potential and intensity domains, changes in stroke rate and stroke length during swims to exhaustion, and arm coordination, to understand how technique adapts under fatigue. It also introduces the idea of an "optimal swimming technique speed" linked to the intensity domains.1

In practice: speed in the water is how many strokes you take per minute times how far you travel per stroke. When you tire, something in that usually changes. So technique should be judged at a stated pace and fatigue state, not in the abstract.

Wetsuit and technique

A review on wetsuits reports that with some wetsuits, compared with a regular swimsuit, stroke rate, stroke length and stroke index are higher, and energy cost is lower with the full-body wetsuit. These are the figures given in the abstract: technique in a wetsuit is not the same as without one.2

What we do not do

We give no target numbers for stroke rate, SWOLF or strokes per length. We did not find them in a source that makes them valid for an amateur, and a number taken from competitive swimmers would mislead. We do not correct technique remotely and do not publish drills as prescriptions: that takes a coach who watches you swim.

What we know and what we do not

We know which quantities are used to describe technique and that they change with fatigue. From these sources we do not know which values are better for an amateur. The evidence is missing, not the willingness to write it.
