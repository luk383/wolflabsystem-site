In brief

  • Carlotta Missaglia won the World Triathlon Cup in Tauranga, New Zealand: the first World Cup podium and victory of her career, taken in a sprint from a group of seven. Sara Guerrero (ESP) took silver four seconds back, Zuzana Michalickova (SVK) bronze.1
  • The 27-year-old Italian (Carabinieri - Valdigne Triathlon), fourth at the last two World Cups (San Pedro de la Paz and Iquique), ran the final 5 km in 16:20, the fastest split of the day.14
  • The men's race went to Izan Edo Aguilar (ESP) in 52:11, ahead of Mitch Kolkman (NED, +3) and Baptiste Passemard (FRA, +4). Alessio Crociani finished fifth 9 seconds back, Pietro Giovannini 26th, Samuele Angelini 32nd with a 10-second penalty flagged by the official timing.2
  • Tauranga hosted a World Cup for the first time; the venue will stage a T50 event in 2027 and the T50 Finals in 2028.1

Missaglia, a first win built in the closing metres

After back-to-back fourth places in Chile, in San Pedro de la Paz and Iquique, Carlotta Missaglia completed the journey at Pilot Bay: she outsprinted six rivals to win the inaugural Tauranga World Cup, the first World Cup podium and victory of her career. Her previous best international result was Mixed Relay silver at WTCS Quiberon in June.1

The race was decided in the final straight: at the last timing point seven athletes were separated by one second (Missaglia, Van Der Kaay, Guerrero, Casals, Kropkó, Daniels and Michalickova). Missaglia covered the last stretch in 3:55, three seconds quicker than Guerrero and seven quicker than Michalickova, crossing the line with her arms raised. Her run split, 16:20 over 5 km, was the fastest of the day. The finishing time, 57:33, is reported by Triatlon Channel; the official online results were not yet published at the time of writing.13

How the women's race unfolded

Kanae Takenaka (JPN) led out of the 750 m swim, four seconds clear of Kelly Wetteland (USA) and Michalickova, with Missaglia ninth but well placed. On the bike a lead group of 11 grew to 21 before the chasers bridged across on the third of four laps: 31 athletes reached the second transition together, led by Emma Jeffcoat (AUS).1

Over the 5 km run the group thinned out: Missaglia, Kropkó and Guerrero set the pace through the first kilometre, then Van Der Kaay, Casals and Juránková moved to the front halfway through, before the decisive sprint. Maria Casals (ESP) finished fourth in 57:44, as in Miyazaki, home favourite Nicole Van Der Kaay (NZL) fifth two seconds back, top seed Márta Kropkó (HUN) sixth three seconds further behind. The 2025 U23 world champion Richelle Hill (AUS) did not start.1

Men's race: Edo Aguilar steals it, Crociani fifth

In the men's race Izan Edo Aguilar (ESP) claimed his first World Cup in 52:11, a week after silver in Miyazaki: according to Triatlon Channel's account, Crociani was first out of the second transition, but a group of seven formed with 2.5 km to go and the Spaniard attacked 400 metres from the line. Mitch Kolkman (NED) finished three seconds back, Baptiste Passemard (FRA) four.23

Roberto Sanchez Mantecon (ESP) was fourth six seconds down, Alessio Crociani fifth nine seconds behind the winner: a top five that confirms his return to form after 12th at the WTCS Finals in Abu Dhabi. The pre-race favourite John Reed (USA) finished eighth, 24 seconds back.2

The other Italians

Pietro Giovannini finished 26th in 53:42, 1:32 behind the winner. Samuele Angelini was 32nd in 54:00 (+1:50): the official timing page flags a 10-second penalty next to his name.2 The four Italians had been selected by the federation for this New Zealand trip, the first World Cup hosted by Tauranga.4

Limitations

This is a results piece based on World Triathlon's race report and the official online timing, both read directly this morning. The full official classification on the event site was not yet available at the time of writing (the results page showed "results not available"): the men's positions and times come from the official live timing page. Missaglia's finishing time (57:33) and some details of the men's race dynamics come from Triatlon Channel, a Spanish specialist outlet, not from an official document. The 10-second penalty flagged for Angelini could change his final position once the official classification is published. We were not present at the venue.
