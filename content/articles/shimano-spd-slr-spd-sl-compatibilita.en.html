In short

  • CL-SL100, CL-SL110, CL-SL120 and CL-SL130 cleats are for SPD-SLR pedals. Shimano lists them as incompatible with SPD-SL pedals: colour alone is not enough.2-5
  • The three bolts on a shoe concern cleat mounting. They do not guarantee that the cleat will engage a particular pedal.
  • The grey CL-SL130 provides adjustment for many traditional three-bolt shoes, but does not turn an SPD-SL pedal into SPD-SLR or retain the core range's stack-height and weight savings.1, 5

The final R changes the system, not just the name

SPD-SLR is not another spelling of SPD-SL. Shimano's road range includes Dura-Ace PD-R9300 and Ultegra PD-R8200 pedals with dedicated cleats. This is a compatibility guide, not news of a launch today: Cyclist covered the system on 17 September 2026.1, 7

Check the full model code on both components. All four SPD-SLR cleat pages explicitly state that they are not compatible with SPD-SL pedals. Cyclist also reports that older SPD-SL cleats are incompatible with SPD-SLR pedals. Do not treat the two families as interchangeable, even when the cleats share a colour.2-5, 7

Shoe, cleat, pedal: two different connections

First there is the cleat's attachment to the sole; then the cleat's engagement with the pedal. A three-bolt shoe may allow mounting without resolving pedal compatibility or whether you can reach the required cleat position.

On Shimano's newest shoes, the manufacturer explains, fore-aft adjustment is handled by the shoe, while CL-SL100/110/120 cleats provide lateral adjustment. That is why "it has three bolts" is not a complete answer: the sole's available adjustment range matters too.2-4

The four SPD-SLR cleats: total float and each direction

Float is the rotational movement available while the cleat is engaged. These are Shimano's stated values, separating total range from movement in each direction: 6° total and ±3° describe the same amount, not two different options. This table covers only the SPD-SLR family.2-5

Red, yellow and blue are reused from the earlier system: they are not evidence of mechanical compatibility. We do not infer which float is best for your knees, or that less movement produces more watts. These are component specifications, not a biomechanical prescription.

CL-SL130: more adjustment, not an adapter

Shimano describes the grey CL-SL130 as intended for non-Shimano shoes and older Shimano models that lack the expanded fore-aft adjustment of its newest shoes. The system page says "most" non-Shimano three-bolt road shoes, not all. Check the specific shoe-cleat pairing and achievable position, rather than counting bolts alone.1, 5

The limitation is explicit: CL-SL130 does not share the stack-height or weight savings of CL-SL100/110/120. It also remains a cleat for SPD-SLR pedals, not a bridge to older SPD-SL pedals.1, 5

Stack height: what 2.3 and 2.8 mm refer to

The PD-R9300 page attributes a 2.3 mm stack-height reduction to the new interface compared with the previous generation. The 2.8 mm figure instead concerns the system together with the S-Phyre RC910 shoe: 2.3 mm from the interface plus 0.5 mm from the shoe, according to Shimano. We do not extend these figures to CL-SL130, for which the manufacturer excludes the core range's advantage.1, 6

These are the manufacturer's dimensional claims, not WOLFLAB measurements of efficiency or speed. They are not an automatic instruction to lower your saddle: a change in a contact point needs to be considered within the whole bike setup.

Before changing components

  • Read the pedal model and system, including on your indoor or second bike.
  • Match it to a cleat declared compatible with that pedal, rather than relying on colour.
  • Check shoe clearance, adjustment and achievable position; seek model-specific confirmation if these are unclear.

Limits

No WOLFLAB engagement, wear or performance test. Shimano sources are US commercial pages: they do not establish Italian pricing, availability or shipping, none of which we report here. In particular, the CL-SL130 page shows conflicting availability signals. We do not certify every shoe-cleat combination or derive clinical benefits from float values.
