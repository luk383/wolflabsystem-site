At a glance

  • The World Aquatics Swimming Championships (25m) run in Beijing on 1-6 December 2026 at the National Aquatics Center, the Water Cube of the 2008 Olympics: more than 1,000 athletes from over 200 countries are expected.2
  • Italy races with 26 swimmers (10 women and 16 men): captain Lorenzo Zazzeri returns, Francesco Volpe, born in 2008, earns a first call-up; Gregorio Paltrinieri is out, long focused on open water.13
  • Watching from Italy: heats at 3:00am, semifinals and finals from 12:00 midday (the final day, Sunday 6, is finals only from 11:00).1

The event

World Aquatics has confirmed the dates: six days of racing, 1-6 December 2026, in the Chinese capital. It is the third time China hosts the short course Worlds after Shanghai 2006 and Hangzhou 2018. The previous edition, Budapest 2024, produced 30 world records across 45 medal events.2

The Italian team

The Italian Swimming Federation has named 26 swimmers, built from the eleven already qualified through the selection criteria plus the names that emerged from the Riccione national championships and the relay assessments. The 10 women: Alessandra Mao, Sara Curtis, Silvia Di Pietro, Emma Virginia Menicucci, Noemi Cesarano, Benedetta Pilato, Simona Quadarella, Costanza Cocconcelli, Anita Gastaldi and Sara Franceschi. The 16 men: Simone Cerasuolo, Carlos D'Ambrosio, Alberto Razzetti, Nicolò Martinenghi, Thomas Ceccon, Lorenzo Zazzeri, Leonardo Deplano, Andrea Battista Candela, Lorenzo Mora, Simone Stefanì, Michele Lamberti, Luca De Tullio, Alessandro Ragaini, Andrea Camozzi, Francesco Volpe and Jacopo Barbotti. Head coach: Cesare Butini.1

Three notes of context. Zazzeri returns to the national team after being left out of the Paris Europeans; Paltrinieri, as expected, skips the short course Worlds, having focused on open water for years.3 Among the latest additions: Lorenzo Mora and Michele Lamberti, both under the world qualifying standard in the 50 backstroke in Riccione (22.75 and 22.79), Emma Virginia Menicucci, who won her first career Italian title in the 100 freestyle there in 52.06, the second-fastest Italian performance ever behind Sara Curtis, and Sara Franceschi, who made the standard in the 400 medley with 4:29.12.4

Where Italy starts from

At Budapest 2024 Italy won nine medals: one gold (the mixed 4×50 freestyle relay with Deplano, Miressi, Di Pietro and Curtis), five silvers and three bronzes; Alberto Razzetti took three individual medals. Of the 26 picked for Beijing, 14 were in Budapest, and from the gold-medal relay Deplano, Di Pietro and Curtis return.14

Schedule and Italian times

Each day has two sessions: heats at 10:00 local (3:00am in Italy) and semifinals/finals at 19:00 local (12:00 midday in Italy); on Sunday 6 December the last session is finals only and starts at 18:00 local (11:00 in Italy).1

  • Tuesday 1: finals of the women's 400 freestyle, 200 medley, 4×100 freestyle and the men's 1500 (fast heat).
  • Wednesday 2: finals of the 100 backstroke, 50 butterfly, women's 800 (fast heat) and the mixed 4×50 medley.
  • Thursday 3: finals of the 100 freestyle, 200 butterfly, 100 breaststroke, men's 400 freestyle and women's 4×200 freestyle.
  • Friday 4: finals of the mixed 4×50 freestyle, 200 breaststroke, 50 backstroke and 100 medley.
  • Saturday 5: finals of the 100 butterfly, 400 medley, men's 800 (fast heat) and mixed 4×100 medley.
  • Sunday 6 (finals only): 50 freestyle, 50 breaststroke, 200 backstroke, 200 freestyle and 4×100 medley.

Limitations

This article is based on the Italian team announcement published by the federation through its official magazine Nuoto.com, on World Aquatics' date confirmation, and on reports by Sky Sport and SwimSwam Italia, all read on 25 November. The federation's own website was unreachable at verification time: the roster and schedule come from the federation's official magazine and match Sky Sport. Final start lists, with each swimmer's event-by-event entries, are not yet published: check the World Aquatics site close to the event for who races what. The sources disagree on the edition number (Nuoto.com calls it the 17th, World Aquatics the 18th), so we do not state one.
