In short

  • On 8 November 2026 Rodrigue Kwizera won the 44th Cross Internacional de Itálica (Santiponce, Seville) in 27:37 over 9.55km: his third victory at the event, matching Kenenisa Bekele.
  • In the women's race Likina Amebaw (Ethiopia) won in 30:41, her first Itálica victory after finishing third in 2025.
  • Full official results are already published by World Athletics: times and places cited here come from that source.

Men's race

Kwizera crossed the line in 27:37, one second ahead of Kenya's Charles Kiboino (27:38) and two ahead of 19-year-old Frankline Kibet (27:39), the world U20 cross-country and 5000m champion making his senior international debut. The trio entered the final 200 metres together before Kwizera decided it in the sprint.1

  1. Rodrigue Kwizera (ESP): 27:37
  2. Charles Kiboino (KEN): 27:38
  3. Frankline Kibet (KEN): 27:39
  4. Denis Kipkemoi (KEN): 27:52
  5. Thierry Ndikumwenayo (ESP): 27:59

For Kwizera it is a third Itálica title after 2021 and 2025: he equals Kenenisa Bekele's record of three wins in the event's roll of honour, which began in 1982. Born in Burundi, Kwizera obtained Spanish nationality in May 2026, and this is his first Itálica win under the Spanish flag.1,3 Berihu Aregawi (ETH) finished ninth in 28:24. Among the Italians, Luca Alfieri was 13th in 28:48 and Nicolò Bedini 21st in 29:13.2

Women's race

Amebaw broke away with a change of pace at the bell and won in 30:41, eight seconds ahead of Uganda's Charity Cherop (30:49). Kenya's Cynthia Chepkirui was third in 30:57.1,2

  1. Likina Amebaw (ETH): 30:41
  2. Charity Cherop (UGA): 30:49
  3. Cynthia Chepkirui (KEN): 30:57
  4. Yenenesh Shimket (ETH): 31:24
  5. Maurine Chebor (KEN): 32:02

The Netherlands' Amina Maatoug was seventh (32:18), the first European across the line; Spain's Carolina Robles was eighth (32:51).2

Context

The race, a Gold-level meeting of the World Athletics Cross Country Tour, was held over a four-lap circuit around the ruins of the ancient Roman city of Itálica, with 5,057 entrants across categories according to the organisers: a figure reported by sources and not independently verifiable.4 Amebaw arrived from the world 5km road title in Copenhagen 2026 and a win at the Amorebieta cross three weeks earlier; she is based in Peguerinos, in the Spanish province of Ávila.1,4
