The calendar sets the priority

The question is not only what to eat after swimming, but how soon the next demanding session begins. Aquatics GB distinguishes refuelling carbohydrate stores, rebuilding and repairing tissues, and replacing fluid. Its guidance pays particular attention to sessions less than eight hours apart. That is not the same context as a short swim followed by a day off. [1]

Before: plan what will be available afterwards

Checking timing, duration and food access makes recovery easier. If an early swim leads straight into work, arranging an accessible meal or snack avoids leaving the plan to chance. This is practical organisation, not a new timing rule or a performance promise.

Aquatics GB offers examples such as yoghurt and fruit, milk and cereal, eggs on toast or a sandwich. These do not imply that a particular recovery product is compulsory. Normal foods and drinks can contribute to the three goals. [1]

During: a sports drink is not automatic

AIS does not consider sports drinks necessary in every session. Its endurance guidance lists 30–60 g carbohydrate/hour for 1–2.5 hours and up to 90 g/hour beyond 2.5–3 hours, using suitable mixtures at higher intakes. These are activity-duration references, not doses already validated for every swimmer or pool set. A short technique session is a different case. [2]

In open water, feeding access, support and rules depend on the event. Do not improvise stops or feeds from a generic table. This guide does not set a protocol for crossings or long-distance races.

After: three goals, no magic stopwatch

GoalUseful question
CarbohydrateWhen is the next demanding session?
ProteinDoes the planned meal contain a protein source?
FluidHow do conditions and losses differ from usual?

Aquatics GB encourages an early start to recovery and gives post-training quantities. Its page is practical team guidance, not proof that benefits disappear at one hour. That advice is not turned here into a rigid window for everyone. The day’s overall intake and sequence of sessions also belong in the plan. [1]

Keep these two contexts separate

Organisational example: after 40 easy minutes with a normal meal already close, an energy drink is not automatically an extra requirement. After hard morning training with another session in the afternoon, planning food and fluid between the sessions becomes more important. These are illustrative scenarios, not personal menus or doses.

Being in water is not a reason to drink without limit. The hyponatraemia consensus identifies excess fluid intake as a risk and does not treat sodium as protection against overdrinking. No fixed bottle count is prescribed here. [3]
