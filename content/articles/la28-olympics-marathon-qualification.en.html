In brief

  • On 10 November 2026 World Athletics published the athletics qualification system for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympic Games, to be held from 15 to 30 July 2028.1
  • The system keeps its dual pathway but changes the balance: about 40% of places go to athletes achieving the entry standards, 60% to the world rankings (the previous model was 50/50). A standard alone does not guarantee a place.1
  • In the marathon the standards are 2:05:00 for men and 2:22:20 for women; the qualification window runs from 1 November 2026 to 30 April 2028.1
  • The first five finishers at Platinum Label marathons in the window are treated as having achieved the standard: the Valencia Marathon on 6 December 2026 is a Platinum Label race in the World Athletics calendar.1,2

The dual pathway: 40% standards, 60% rankings

As at recent outdoor global championships, athletes qualify in two ways: by hitting the entry standard or through the world rankings. The split changes: from the previous 50/50 model to roughly 40% of quota places through standards and 60% through rankings.1

One caveat that matters: if more athletes than the event quota achieve the standard, qualification is limited to the best performances in the period, within the quota, and in that case nobody qualifies through the rankings. A standard is not an automatic ticket.1

To set the standards, World Athletics used the qualification periods of the last four global championships (the 2022, 2023 and 2025 World Championships and the 2024 Olympics) with three athletes per country, factoring in trends and expected improvements. For the marathon it states that in an Olympic year times run 30 to 60 seconds faster than trends would forecast: hence the tougher standards.1

Marathon: standards, window and the 19 March 2028 rule

The standards are 2:05:00 for men and 2:22:20 for women, to be achieved between 1 November 2026 and 30 April 2028.1

19 March 2028 is the key date: any athlete ranked above the 56th athlete on the filtered "Road to LA" quota list (maximum three per national Olympic committee) on that day is considered qualified. After that date the remaining 15 places are allocated through the same dual pathway, without displacing those already qualified.1

There is also a big-race shortcut: the first five finishers at Platinum Label marathons held during the whole qualification period are considered as having achieved the standard and are listed first on the "Road to LA", the official monitoring tool World Athletics will publish in the latter part of 2027.1

Finally, any national Olympic committee may reallocate a quota place to an unqualified athlete, provided they have run at least 2:11:00 (men) or 2:29:00 (women) within the window.1

What this means for Valencia

The Valencia Marathon on 6 December 2026 falls inside the qualification window and is a Platinum Label race according to the World Athletics calendar: the first five athletes across the line effectively earn the Olympic standard.1,2,3 The field includes names like Yomif Kejelcha, whose half-marathon world record was ratified on 9 November.

Valencia 2026 is also the last edition carrying the one-million-euro prize announced by Juan Roig for a world record: we covered it in Valencia Marathon 2026: the last call for the million euros.

Be careful not to stack the two: the top-five rule grants the standard, not a guaranteed place. The quota mechanism, the maximum of three athletes per nation and the 19 March 2028 ranking still apply.1

The other running events: windows and standards

The windows differ by discipline: for the 10,000m, combined events, race walks and relays they run from 19 December 2026 to 18 June 2028; for all other events from 19 June 2027 to 18 June 2028. Results from competitions whose last day falls within the window count in full.1

Race walks and relays have their own shortcuts too: the first 12 at the World Athletics Race Walking Team Championships in Hefei (9 April 2028) qualify for the half marathon race walk, and the first 14 national committees at the World Athletics Relays in the Bahamas (22-23 April 2028) book their relay places.1 The Olympic programme will feature 23 women's events, 23 men's events and two mixed events, with the Olympic debut of the mixed 4x100m relay.1
