Why the AERO Elite is new

On 1 October Skechers introduced the AERO Elite as its entry into the super-shoe segment. That is the manufacturer's definition: a lightweight racing shoe for distances from 5K to the marathon, with HYPER BURST PRO foam, a full-length carbon-infused Quantum Plate and HYPER ARC rocker geometry.1

The Italian AERO Series page places it above the daily-training and speed models: not the shoe for every kilometre, but the specific race-day model.4 Triatlón Noticias covered the Spanish presentation on 12 November and aimed it at triathletes looking for a light shoe for the run leg. That report also says the claimed performance still needs long-term testing.6

The first wear test read: four opinions, not a verdict

Believe in the Run published a four-tester review on 13 November. It is an independent wear test, not a lab measurement: each tester's foot, pace and route matters.5

TesterReported useMain outcome
KalebNearly 40 miles, including very fast workVery positive: true-to-size secure upper, fast feel and useful from 5K to the marathon for experienced runners. He punctured an exposed midsole area on gravel but saw no other wear.
SamA 23-mile long runDisappointed: the forefoot felt heavy, turnover felt less fluid and the upper was hard to lock down. He reported bloody blisters.
MegWorkouts with pickups and marathon-pace segmentsPositive about softness and rebound, including during warm-up and cool-down. She wanted more heel foam at slower paces.
ChadThree fast sessions during his New York City Marathon buildEasy turnover and a plate that did not feel too aggressive, but the minimal heel felt unstable to him: he would not use it beyond 10K or a half marathon.

The shared point is not "it works for everyone". The AERO Elite seems to come alive more as pace rises. Runners who land heavily on the heel, want stability at easy paces or need a highly adjustable fit should try it before considering it for a marathon.

Who it may suit

For a trained runner who wants a light racing shoe and likes rocker geometries, the AERO Elite is worth following: the Italian price is explicit and early wear reports suggest strong potential at faster paces. For someone looking for a stable daily shoe, a first plated shoe or a marathon option they have not tested on long runs and workouts, the picture is less clear.

The tester split is useful. The same upper felt secure to Kaleb and problematic to Sam; the same platform felt fast to Chad, but too unstable for him beyond shorter races. Before spending €245, size and foot strike matter more than the technology name.

Limitations

WOLFLAB has not tested the AERO Elite. Weight, stack, technologies and price were verified on Skechers pages; Italian availability is a snapshot of the pages checked on 17 November and can change by size, colour and channel. The Believe in the Run review is independent but subjective: it does not provide instrumental measurements of rebound, stability or durability.
