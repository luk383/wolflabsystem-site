In short

  • Around 80% of sessions, or more, should be at low intensity: you can talk without effort.
  • The rest is split between threshold work and hard work. Polarised and pyramidal are the two observed patterns; neither is proven better for everyone.
  • The most common amateur mistake is running or riding at moderate intensity too often.

Three zones, not seven

To think about distribution, three zones are enough. Below the first threshold (zone 1) you can talk easily. Between the first and second threshold (zone 2) you speak in short phrases: that's marathon pace and steady running. Above the second threshold (zone 3) you can only manage a few words: that's fast intervals.

What the best athletes do

Studying high-level rowers, cross-country skiers, runners and cyclists, Stephen Seiler, reviewing descriptive studies of national and international level athletes training 10 to 13 times a week, found a recurring pattern: about 80% of sessions at low intensity and 20% dominated by high-intensity work. It describes what these athletes do; it does not prove it is the best pattern.1 Hence the name "polarised": little work in the middle zone.

A systematic review of 10 studies of highly trained and elite distance runners found that they typically follow a pyramidal model: lots of zone 1, a smaller share of zone 2, even less zone 3.2 Marathoners used the pyramidal pattern more often. The common ground stays the same: easy work is always the foundation.

In a 9-week study of trained endurance athletes, the polarised model produced the largest gain in VO₂peak (+11.7%) and in peak power or speed compared with threshold, volume or high-intensity interval training. It was a single study: 48 athletes, 9 weeks, intensity controlled by heart rate, no difference in work economy and no race result measured.3

In practice

The points below are rules of thumb inspired by these studies, not protocols tested on amateurs.

  • With 5 sessions a week: 4 easy, 1 quality. With 6–7 sessions you can go up to 2 quality sessions.
  • Easy sessions must be truly easy. If you can't talk, slow down: walking uphill is fine.
  • For the marathon race pace falls in zone 2: in the specific phase work at that pace increases, and the model becomes more pyramidal.
  • It applies to the bike too: long, easy rides count as zone 1 work in the week's total.
