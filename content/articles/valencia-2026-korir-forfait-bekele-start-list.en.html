At a glance

  • John Korir, winner of the 2025 edition in 2:02:24, will not run the Valencia Marathon on 6 December: organizers announced his injury withdrawal on 23 November, without disclosing the injury or a recovery timeline.23
  • His exit adds to Yomif Kejelcha's: the Ethiopian had planned a world record attempt in Valencia, so the race loses the two most anticipated names on the men's bill.23
  • On the updated official start list the fastest man is Kenenisa Bekele (2:01:41), with five more men under 2:04; Fotyen Tesfay (2:10:51) leads the women's list, which also includes reigning world half-marathon champion Agnes Ngetich on her marathon debut.19

What happened

The race's official account announced on Monday 23 November that John Korir cannot defend the title he won in 2025: the 29-year-old Kenyan decided to withdraw with an injury whose nature and recovery time were not disclosed. In Valencia last year Korir won in 2:02:247, and in April he took Boston in 2:01:52, a course record6; he is the 10th-fastest marathoner ever.24

His withdrawal follows Yomif Kejelcha's: the Ethiopian, who finished second on his marathon debut in London 2026 in 1:59:41, behind Sabastian Sawe's 1:59:30 world record5, was chasing exactly that record in Valencia and with it the one-million-euro prize offered by Juan Roig, which, as covered in our November preview, expires with this edition.23

Who is left: the official start list

From the entry list published by the organizers (checked on 25 November), the numerical favourite is Kenenisa Bekele, wearing bib 2 with a 2:01:41 personal best. The other men under 2:04 are Birhanu Legese (2:02:48), Alexander Mutiso (2:03:11), Vincent Ngetich (2:03:13), Geofry Toirotich (2:03:30) and Dawit Wolde (2:03:48); just outside the mark sit Nibret Melak (2:04:00) and Leul Gebreselassie (2:04:02). Ngetich makes his Valencia debut after winning the Jakarta half marathon on 24 October in 1:01:04. Canada's Cam Levins (2:05:36) is also entered.14

The women and the record

With the men's field cut down, world-record attention shifts to the women. On the official list, checked again on 28 November, the fastest is Fotyen Tesfay with 2:10:51, run in Barcelona in March on her marathon debut: according to World-Track's preview it is the second-fastest women's marathon ever, 55 seconds off the record. Behind her are Joyciline Jepkosgei (2:14:00) and Sutume Kebede (2:15:55).18

Making her marathon debut is Agnes Ngetich, bib 303, listed with a half-marathon best of 1:03:04: the 25 November version of this article did not find her on the list and flagged her participation for verification closer to the race. According to World Athletics, Ngetich is the reigning world half-marathon champion: on 20 September in Copenhagen she won the title in 1:05:15, a world record in a women-only race, and she also holds the world 10km road records (28:46 in a mixed race, 29:27 in a women-only race) and the 2026 world cross-country title.19

The world record to beat officially remains Ruth Chepngetich's 2:09:56 (Chicago 2024): the AIU banned her for three years, but the mark was not annulled because the test after that race came back negative, and as of October 2026 it still stood, amid criticism from World Athletics president Coe. Whoever breaks the record in Valencia will collect the one-million-euro prize from the Fundación Trinidad Alfonso which, as covered in our November preview, expires with this edition; according to World-Track, international elite coordinator Marc Roig said that for the first time Valencia can say the world record is the goal.81011 The race runs on Sunday 6 December, starting at 8:15am, with about 36,000 entrants and, per the preview, 63 men with personal bests under 2:10; the men's course record remains Sisay Lemma's 2:01:48 (2023).18

Limitations

This article is based on the official start list on the race website and on reports by Canadian Running, AS and The Star, all read on 25 November. The organizers' social post announcing Korir's withdrawal was not viewed directly: the quote reported is as transcribed by the journalistic sources, including AS with the full text. The nature and prognosis of Korir's injury were not disclosed. The start list can change until race day. Update of 28 November 2026: Agnes Ngetich now appears on the official entry list (bib 303, half-marathon best of 1:03:04) and the women's section has been updated with her honours verified on World Athletics, the current status of Chepngetich's record (still standing) and World-Track's preview; in the 25 November version her entry still needed verification. The 1:03:04 best comes from the entry list: the women-only world record is Copenhagen's 1:05:15, a different measure.
