The format and the schedule

The Long Distance World Championships close the Multisport World Championships in Abu Dhabi (13-22 November) on Sunday 22 November, on Hudayriyat Island. The distance is unique on the calendar, sitting between middle and full distance: a 3 km swim over two laps, 120 km on the bike over four laps and a 30 km run. The official schedule has the elite men starting at 7:00 local (4:00 CET), the elite women at 7:05 (4:05 CET) and the para categories at 7:06; elite medal ceremonies are scheduled for 14:30 local (11:30 CET). The race will be streamed live on TriathlonLive and YouTube.123

Men: Benito for the three-peat, Draper for the comeback

Benito Lopez has made this title his own: second on his debut in Ibiza in 2023, he won in Townsville in 2024 by more than three minutes and defended the crown in Pontevedra by more than seven, running the final 30 km in 1:48:50. He arrives off a 10th place in Dubai last Saturday: his preparation for the heat and the faster end of long distance racing is fresh, even if his legs may not be.1

The most credible challenger is William Draper: bronze in Pontevedra, where he posted the fastest bike split of any finisher (2:56:56, 32 seconds quicker than Benito) but gave away more than four minutes in the swim and three and a half on the run. Also in the field: Lukas Kocar (CZE), a minute off the podium in Pontevedra; Ondrej Kubo (SVK), sixth with the second-fastest ride of the day; Wilhelm Hirsch (GER), bringing T100 experience; and Thor Bendix Madsen (DEN), silver at the European Long Distance Championships in Almere-Amsterdam in September.1

Women: Pierré against Pohle

Marjolaine Pierré has won every Long Distance world title she has contested since 2023: her first in Ibiza in 5:53:35, then in Pontevedra, where she rode away from the field (3:11:51, more than nine minutes faster than anyone else) and won by more than 11 minutes. On Sunday she hunts a third crown in four editions.1

The leading challenger is Caroline Pohle, in the form of her life over the T100 format: fourth in Dubai last Saturday, after 10th at the T100 World Championship Final in Qatar last December and fifth in Oropesa in September 2025. On her full-distance debut at this year's Challenge Roth, however, she hit the wall 30 km into the run. France fields three athletes: alongside Pierré, Aurelia Boulanger (who did not finish in Pontevedra) and Anne-Sophie Pierre, a top-10 finisher in Kona this year. Sophie Fischer (GER) is doubling up: she raced Friday's Aquathlon World Championships as a warm-up.1

No Italians on the start line

The official start lists count 19 elite men and 12 elite women: a full check of both tables shows no athlete of Italian nationality. The Italian delegation in Abu Dhabi raced the other distances in the programme. In the para field, start lists cover the PTWC, PTS2-5 and PTVI classes for men and women, starting at 7:06.2

Limitations

This is a preview based on World Triathlon's official preview, start lists and published schedule, not on our presence at the venue. Accounts of past results (margins, splits, placings) are those of the official preview. The winners' finishing time is not stated by the organisers: the 14:30 local medal ceremonies are a schedule item, not an estimate of race times. Final weather conditions will only be known at the start. Start lists can change until the race begins. The stated broadcast channels (TriathlonLive and YouTube) are those indicated by World Triathlon.
