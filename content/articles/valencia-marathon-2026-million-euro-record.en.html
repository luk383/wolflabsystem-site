In short

  • The 46th Valencia Marathon (Platinum Label) runs on 6 December 2026. Race patron Juan Roig has announced it will be the last edition carrying his one-million-euro prize for a world record.12
  • The prize, announced in December 2023, goes to whoever - man or woman - breaks the marathon world record in Valencia. It has never been won.23
  • The men's mark to beat is 1:59:30 by Sabastian Sawe (London, 26 April 2026), ratified by World Athletics on 23 July 2026.4 The absolute women's record is 2:09:56 by Ruth Chepngetich (Chicago 2024).2
  • The leading candidate is Yomif Kejelcha: second in London in 1:59:41 and half-marathon world record holder (56:51) in Buenos Aires on 23 August 2026, pending ratification.56

What the one-million-euro prize is

After the 2023 edition, Juan Roig - president of the Fundación Trinidad Alfonso and the race's main backer - announced an extraordinary prize: one million euros for anyone who broke the marathon world record on the streets of Valencia, man or woman. According to Marca's account, the sum would be put up half by Roig and half by then race director Paco Borao.3

The prize has never been awarded since. On 25 October 2026, after the Valencia half marathon, Roig gave it a deadline: "This is the last year to take the million euros. Next year it ends - I will remove the million-euro sponsorship."1 The marathon on 6 December 2026 is therefore the final chance.2

One important caveat: the detailed conditions of the prize are not published in any accessible rulebook. The one reported by sources is "break the world record in Valencia"; we do not know whether ratification by World Athletics is required or how the women's record is treated (absolute or "women only"). We flag this because the prize should not be read as automatic at a given clock time: it is a sponsor's announcement, as reported by the press.12

Running under 1:59:30 means covering 42.195 km at about 2:50 per kilometre: that is halfway in roughly 59:45 and holding it to the line. In London Sawe managed it by dropping Kejelcha in the final mile; the Ethiopian finished in 1:59:41, the second man ever under two hours in a record-eligible race, with Jacob Kiplimo third in 2:00:28.45

Kejelcha is the only entrant who has already run under two hours: to break the record he must improve his 1:59:41 by at least 12 seconds, about 0.3 seconds per kilometre. His form is measurable: on 23 August in Buenos Aires he regained the half-marathon world record with 56:51, 29 seconds under Kiplimo's 57:20 (both performances were pending ratification at the date of the World Athletics report).6 Among the women, Tesfay is 56 seconds off Chepngetich's absolute record; Jepkosgei, the 2025 winner here, holds the course record.78

Roig is backing Kejelcha: "That he chose Valencia to break the record shows great knowledge, because Valencia has the ideal circuit to do it."1

Why Valencia

The course is completely flat and at sea level, on wide avenues; in December the temperature is usually between 12 and 17 °C. With Lemma's 2:01:48 it is the fourth-fastest marathon in the world according to the race website, and it holds the Platinum Label, World Athletics' highest.8 That the circuit is genuinely fast is shown by the course records: the men's is the sixth-fastest performance ever, the women's the fourth.8
