In brief

  • Weekly program for people who have used up per-session linear progression: volume on Monday, recovery on Wednesday, intensity on Friday.
  • Described by Mark Rippetoe (2013). No study evaluates it.
  • The numbers in the source are for a specific lifter: the example below is illustrative, not a plan.

Structure of the week

DaySquatPushPull
Monday, volume5×5 at about 90% of 5RMBench and overhead press, alternating weeks: 5×5 at about 90% of 5RMDeadlift: one heavy set of 5, after squats and push
Wednesday, recovery2×5 at 80% of Monday's weightBench 3 lighter sets, or light overhead press (alternating)Chin-ups (3 sets to failure) and back extensions
Friday, intensityNew 5RM or within 2%Bench or overhead press at a higher load than MondayPower clean 5×3 or power snatch 6×2

The source explains that 5×5 at constant load is, for the author, the best combination of volume and intensity, and that the weight should be completable with rests of up to 8–10 minutes. Deadlifts have no volume day because, he writes, you cannot recover from more than one heavy set.1

Illustrative example

Squat, starting from a 5RM of 140 kg. Made-up numbers to show the mechanism, not a prescription.

DaySquat
Monday5×5 at about 125 kg (90% of 140 kg, rounded to plates)
Wednesday2×5 at about 100 kg (80% of 125 kg)
Fridayone set of 5 at the new maximum: 140 kg or within 2% (about 137.5 kg), after ramping warm-up sets

The source's own example, in pounds: with a 5RM of 345 lb, Monday would be 315 lb × 5 × 5.1

Why this shape

According to the source, at some point you can no longer add weight every session. The volume work creates the stimulus, the light session helps recovery and the intensity session turns the work into a new 5-rep maximum. This is the author's explanation, not an experimental result.1

Limits

  • It is designed for lifters who are past the beginner phase. The source describes it as a hard program: it warns that Monday will wreck the week if recovery, sleep and food are not enough. That makes it a poor fit on its own for people adding a lot of running or cycling: compatibility not assessed.
  • It is not a universal plan: the text does not say what to do if a week goes badly. Many variants share the name.
  • The power clean needs technique. If you replace it, the routine is “inspired by”.
  • No controlled study evaluates this program. For the general evidence on loading see Strength, size and power.
