At a glance

  • On Friday 13 November Shimano told its OEM partners that lead times for affected models were switching to "TBD". Escape Collective reported it first, and Shimano confirmed it in a statement.1
  • According to the statement, supply of "some" Di2 and e-bike products is affected by a shortage of printed circuit boards (PCBs); for the affected models, new promised delivery dates are suspended. It is not described as a total shipping halt.2
  • Models, volumes and duration have not been disclosed. The wider causes (AI data centre demand, conflict in the Middle East) are the journalists' reconstruction, not a Shimano statement.12

What was said

Escape Collective reports that on 13 November Shimano wrote to the brands that spec its parts on complete bikes: for affected models, lead times became "TBD". Shimano then confirmed the suspension to Escape Collective and other outlets.1

The statement as quoted by Pinkbike says supply of some Di2 and e-bike products is affected by PCB shortages, that new promised delivery dates are suspended for the affected models, and that Shimano will resume giving dates and updated lead-time estimates once it has "sufficient visibility" of the supply situation. Shimano adds that mechanical component supply is not affected by PCB availability.2

What was not said

In the sources we read there is no list of delayed models or codes, no volumes and no estimate of duration. Bikeitalia stresses that, for now, this is not described as a total shipping halt.3 Pinkbike says SRAM had not replied to its request for comment and that, according to unofficial sources, it has told partners it has no concerns: that is unofficial, not an SRAM statement.2

The causes, attributed

Escape Collective links the PCB shortage to AI data centre demand and the war with Iran; Pinkbike cites Electronics Weekly as saying PCB lead times and prices have stretched globally because of data centre demand and supply constraints linked to the Middle East conflict. Shimano, in the statement quoted, names only the shortage of circuit boards and does not confirm these causes.12

What it could mean for buyers

Nothing we read says which bikes or groupsets are involved, or when shops will see delays. Bikeitalia suggests the effect may depend on stock already in the supply chain and that brands may need to revisit 2027 range schedules: that is the outlet's assessment, not data.3 If you are choosing a bike with Di2 or a Shimano motor, the practical question for the retailer is whether that specific model has a confirmed delivery date.

Limits

This is a summary of press reports and of a Shimano statement quoted by them: we did not see the communications sent to OEM partners. From the Escape Collective page we could read the introduction and first paragraphs; the rest was not available in the version we accessed, and what is reported on causes and other brands relies on Pinkbike and Bikeitalia. We did not check individual models or delivery times with retailers.
