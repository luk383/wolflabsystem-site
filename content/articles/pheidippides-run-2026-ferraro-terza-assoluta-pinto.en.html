At a glance

  • From Thursday 19 to Monday 23 November the tenth Authentic Pheidippides Run was held in Greece, on the route of the messenger Pheidippides: two distances, the classic Athens-Sparta-Athens (about 485 km in the full results list published by iRun) and, for the tenth anniversary, an extended version continuing to Marathon (524.9 km).23
  • In the long race Russia's Ivan Zaborsky won in 67:34:03; Francesca Ferraro (Arenzano, born 1991) was the first woman and third overall in 87:30:56.134
  • In the Athens-Sparta-Athens, Belgium's Stijn De Corte won in 73:06:23; the first woman was Denmark's Stine Rex, fourth overall. Maria Pinto (Como) finished 28th overall and second woman in 96:12:39. Dean Karnazes was among the 54 who did not finish.2

Two races in one for the tenth edition

The event retraces the journey that, according to the historian Herodotus, the messenger Pheidippides made in 490 BC: from Athens to Sparta to ask for help against the Persians, before the battle of Marathon. The race follows that route and adds the return to Athens.1 For the tenth edition, athletes meeting the entry requirements could continue beyond Athens to Marathon: Cronache Ponentine reports a total distance of 524.9 km and a 100-hour time limit, which expired at 10 am on Monday 23 November. The classic distance is about 485 km according to iRun's full results list, with a 98-hour limit. The start was at 6 am on Thursday 19 November in Athens; the course passes through Eleusis, Megara, Corinth, Nemea, Mount Artemisio, Tegea and Sparta.23

The long race to Marathon: Zaborsky and Ferraro

According to Marathonec, Ivan Zaborsky won the extended version in 67:34:03, finishing on the night between Saturday 21 and Sunday 22 November with a wide margin; the same source reports over 6,800 metres of elevation gain and recalls that Zaborsky had already won the 2024 edition on the classic distance, setting the course record.4

Francesca Ferraro, from Arenzano and racing for Emozioni Sport Team, finished in 87:30:56: first woman and third overall, and the only Italian finisher in the long race according to Runner's World, which reports 12 finishers in total on the extended distance. In 2022 Ferraro had already won the women's classification of the Athens-Sparta-Athens (about 490 km at the time), finishing sixth overall.13

The Athens-Sparta-Athens: De Corte, Rex and Pinto

According to the full results list published by iRun, 85 athletes started the classic distance and 31 finished; 54 dropped out, including Greek-American Dean Karnazes. Belgium's Stijn De Corte won in 73:06:23, ahead of Russia's Sergey Tsumakov (73:51:48) and Joseph Faeldonia of the Philippines (77:52:36). The first woman was Denmark's Stine Rex, fourth overall in 78:24:02. The last finisher was 62-year-old Frenchman Thierry Triconnet in 97:39:04, inside the 98-hour limit.2

Maria Pinto, from Como, finished 28th overall in 96:12:39, the second woman across the line after Rex: together with Ferraro, two Italian women stood on the podium of their respective races. Across the two distances, Runner's World counts 43 finishers and 4 women.12

Limitations

This article summarises four journalistic sources read on 28 November 2026, in Italian, Greek and Russian: the results page of the race's official website was not readable at the time of consultation. The positions and times for the Athens-Sparta-Athens come from the full results list published by iRun, which we read in full; we did not cross-check them against a second source. For the long race, in the sources we examined we found no complete classification: we report the winner and Ferraro's result as described by the reports cited, and the second and third men are not named in the sources we read, so we do not report them. The sources disagree on the distances and the actual measurement remains to be confirmed on the official website: iRun and Cronache Ponentine report about 485 km for the Athens-Sparta-Athens and 524.9 km for the extended version, while Runner's World writes 490 and 525 km and reports that the long race's finish was moved after about 510 km because of authorisation problems, with no confirmation in the other sources we examined. Runner's World also lists Pinto as fifth overall, while iRun's full results place her 28th: we used the complete list. Update of 28 November 2026: this paragraph was reworded to make clear that the classic distance results list was read in full and that the actual distances remain to be confirmed on the official source.
