In short

  • Yeman Crippa finished second at the 2026 New York City Marathon in 2:06:14, behind Sweden's Andreas Almgren (2:06:01), who was making his debut at the distance.56
  • For Italy it is the best placing in New York since Giacomo Leone's win in 1996.11 The fastest Italian time on this course had been Stefano Baldini's 2:09:12 from 2002.12
  • Kenya's Sheila Chepkirui won the women's race in 2:24:36. Italy's Giovanna Epis finished eleventh in 2:28:55.6
  • This page started as a course guide: below the results you will find how the five-borough race is laid out.

How the men's race unfolded

The race was run on Sunday 1 November 2026, the edition marking 50 years of the five-borough course, in dry and sunny conditions.8 The lead group stayed together for a long time: the first man passed halfway in 1:04:01, with Almgren second in 1:04:04 and the first 23 men within 10 seconds. At 35 km there were still 15 in contention.8 Between 35 and 40 km Almgren ran 14:26, and at 40 km, reached in 2:00:00, only three remained: him, Crippa and defending champion Benson Kipruto.8

Kipruto was the first to drop back. Almgren and Crippa fought for the win until about 800 metres from the finish, when the Swede pulled decisively clear: he covered the last 2,195 metres in 6:01 and won in 2:06:01, running the second half in 1:01:57.8 Crippa arrived 13 seconds later, in 2:06:14.7 Lesotho's Kamohelo Mofolo was third, passing Kipruto at the end: 2:06:26 to 2:06:27.6 Almgren, the European 10,000 m champion according to ANSA, is the first Swede to win a Marathon Major.59

The top five, according to Runner's World:6

  1. Andreas Almgren (Sweden), 2:06:01
  2. Yeman Crippa (Italy), 2:06:14
  3. Kamohelo Mofolo (Lesotho), 2:06:26
  4. Benson Kipruto (Kenya), 2:06:27
  5. Haftu Strintzos (Australia), 2:06:34

What it means for Italy

According to Gazzetta dello Sport, Crippa is the seventh Italian on the New York City Marathon podium; the last was Eyob Faniel, third in 2021 in 2:09:52.10 For La Stampa it is the best Italian placing since Giacomo Leone's win in 1996, when Crippa had just been born.11

The time matters too. According to the all-time list of Italians under 3 hours in New York published by Queen Atletica, the benchmark on this course was the 2:09:12 with which Stefano Baldini finished fifth in 2002.12 Crippa lowers it by almost three minutes. In April 2026 the Italian had won the Paris Marathon in 2:05:18, his personal best at the distance.13

The women's race

Sheila Chepkirui won in 2:24:36, her second New York title after 2024.57 American Jessica McClain finished second in 2:24:42, passing Hellen Obiri (2:24:43), the defending champion and course record holder, in the last 200 metres: according to Marathon Handbook, McClain is the first American woman on the podium since 2018.14 Giovanna Epis, on her New York debut, finished eleventh in 2:28:55.6

The course in one line

Staten Island, Brooklyn, Queens, Manhattan, the Bronx, Manhattan again. That is the sequence shown on NYRR's official 2026 course map, with the Queensboro Bridge between Queens and Manhattan, Harlem and the Bronx further on, and the finish in Central Park.4 The official 2026 course page says the 26.2-mile race runs through the five boroughs.3

For the start and the kind of course we rely on the NYRR 2025 runner guide, that is the previous edition: the start is on Staten Island at the foot of the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge and the finish is in Central Park.2 We did not read an equivalent 2026 guide, so we do not attribute other details of that guide to the 2026 edition.

Why five boroughs

According to the 2025 NYRR guide, the race began in 1970 with 127 runners circling Central Park and went citywide in 1976. The guide says 2026 marks 50 years of the five-borough course.2 In 2024 the marathon had more than 55,000 finishers and was the largest in the world, again according to the 2025 guide.2
