In brief

  • 5/3/1 is a Jim Wendler system built on a “training max” and on advancing with controlled technique.
  • The official beginner page proposes full-body training three times a week with two main lifts per session.
  • The weekly percentages of 5/3/1 are not on the official pages reviewed: they are not reported.

The beginner variant

Wendler usually tells everyone to do the program as is, whatever their experience. For beginners training without guidance he recommends the basic program. He allows one modification: full-body training three days a week, with two main lifts per session instead of one.1

DayMain liftsThen
MondaySquat and bench, with 5/3/1 sets and 5×5 “First Set Last” (FSL)Assistance work
WednesdayDeadlift and press, same setupAssistance work
FridayBench and squat, same setupAssistance work

The exact meaning of “First Set Last” and of the percentages by week is defined in the 5/3/1 book, not on the page read.

Training max and rep quality

All percentages are calculated on the training max, not the real max. For the beginner variant the page gives 80–85% of the max.1 A post reproduced on the site (written by another forum user) says 90% was the recommended starting point and that the training max adapts to goals.2

Wendler's rule: no rep should be slow, grinding or with poor technique. If a rep fails that standard during the cycle, you do not increase the training max.1

Illustrative example

Squat with an estimated max of 100 kg: a training max at 85% is 85 kg and every percentage is calculated on 85 kg. Made-up numbers to show the mechanism.

Assistance work

Each day one exercise per category (push, pull, single leg or core), with 50–100 total reps per exercise, choosing the sets you like. Starting standards given for a beginner: 10 chin-ups, 35 push-ups, 20 dips, dumbbell squats at half body weight for 20 reps.1

Conditioning

On free days the page includes running or conditioning, with a warning not to overdo it: it follows the 5/3/1 “50% rule”, which the page does not explain. How much endurance load fits needs care: compatibility with a running or cycling plan not assessed.1

Limits

  • The source is the author's own site, which sells the books. It describes the system and does not evaluate it with a study.
  • This is only the beginner variant: the basic 5/3/1 has other setups (for example the four-week cycle) described in the book, which was not read.
  • For the general evidence on loading and volume see Strength, size and power.
