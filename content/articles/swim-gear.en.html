Start with the function

Pool and open-water equipment address different needs. Goggles need to support vision; a wetsuit should fit without restricting the stroke; a visible cap and tow float help make a swimmer noticeable. None of these objects makes a location or condition safe on its own. Swim England’s guides are practical advice, not comparative product tests. [1][2]

Wetsuit: check shoulders, neck, wrists and ankles

Swim England recommends trying before buying where possible. Moving the arms in the suit should not put strong pressure on the shoulders. Wrists, neck and ankles are key fit and water-entry points. The right size cannot be established from height alone. [1]

A higher price does not establish better personal fit. Comfort, freedom and fit come before a generic speed promise. Permitted thickness and mandatory or prohibited use depend on the event’s current rules: old temperature thresholds are not repeated here as current rules.

Goggles: see the water you are swimming in

Swim England distinguishes clear lenses for lower light from darker or mirrored options for bright conditions, asking swimmers to consider the environment. Fit and seal remain individual. A dark lens does not improve visibility when light is lacking; a pool choice is not automatically suitable for a crossing. This guide assigns no performance advantage to a brand. [2]

Tow float and cap: visibility is not rescue

A tow float is a visibility aid, not a lifesaving device. It does not replace support, a supervised location or assessing conditions. RNLI advises suitable locations, checking weather, tides and currents, and not swimming alone. A float is not permission to exceed your abilities offshore. [2][3]

Cold water and sudden entry can compromise breathing and swimming ability. A wetsuit does not remove those risks. Safety decisions belong before entry, not in extra accessories during a problem. [3]

A checklist, not a ranking

  • Pool: check goggle seal and the centre’s rules.
  • Open water: choose the location, support and conditions before the equipment.
  • Wetsuit: test movement and contact points, then check event rules.
  • Tow float: distinguish visibility claims from buoyancy or lifesaving certification.
