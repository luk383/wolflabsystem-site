In short

  • hDrop announced its Strava integration on 13 November, and it is now available in the hDrop app.1
  • After analysis in hDrop, a summary is added to the matching Strava activity description: total sweat loss, sodium and potassium when those results are available. Existing notes stay intact.2
  • This is not a live feed: it needs an internet connection, the activity already on Strava and the matching workout analysis opened in hDrop.2
  • An independent abstract in 19 people found agreement for fluid loss, but an average overestimate of sweat sodium concentration. Do not convert one reading automatically into a salt dose.5

What changes in the Strava activity

The update is not a new sensor. It changes where the results appear. If you use hDrop Gen 2, the app can add a short summary to the description of an activity already in your Strava log. The official page lists three values: total sweat loss, sodium loss and potassium loss, with the caveat that available results are the ones shown.2

hDrop says the summary is added without replacing existing notes. The practical benefit is seeing the value next to distance, pace or power when reviewing the session, without copying it manually from another app.2

Connection and sync

hDrop's route is Account → Connected Apps → Strava, followed by the authorization step. Once the activity is available on Strava, open the analysis for that workout in hDrop: the available results sync to the matching activity description. An internet connection is required.2

Read the permission screen before authorizing. Strava's documentation explains that a third-party app may receive activity read access and, depending on the requested permission level, access to private activities, hidden map portions or upload and edit functions. We are not attributing every one of those scopes to hDrop: check the scopes shown on your own screen and revoke access if you do not consider them necessary.4

A useful reading is not an automatic prescription

The sensor is worn on the arm and the app returns estimates for sweat and electrolytes. hDrop presents Gen 2 as a sports and training product, not a medical device: the product page explicitly says it should not diagnose or treat medical conditions.3

Even an accurate loss measurement does not automatically equal how much you should drink or how many milligrams of sodium you should take. Duration, intensity, climate, diet, health and repeated measurements all change the answer. Use the summary to compare similar sessions, not as a universal dose after every workout.

The independent study: fluids and sodium have different results

An abstract published in Physiology in May 2026 evaluated hDrop in 19 active adults, using two trials 48 hours apart in an environmental chamber at 27 °C and 50% relative humidity. Each trial lasted one hour on a treadmill or cycle ergometer. For fluid loss, hDrop reported 1.0 ± 0.3 litres versus 1.1 ± 0.4 litres from the laboratory method, a non-significant difference in this sample.5

Sodium is the important limitation. hDrop's estimated concentration was higher than the patch and laboratory analyzer measurement: mean bias 12 ± 11 mmol/L, summarized by the authors as about 11 mmol/L. Their abstract warns that an overestimate could lead to sodium intake above actual losses. The study is small, covers one environmental condition and is a conference abstract without full editorial peer review. It does not prove sensor accuracy in every climate, duration or athlete.5

What we did not verify

We have not tested hDrop Gen 2 or repeated the Strava sync. We have not verified price, Italian availability, minimum app version, summary timing or behavior with duplicate and edited activities. This is an explanation of the announced integration, not a WOLFLAB review or medical advice.
